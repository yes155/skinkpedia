# Sources & Research Methodology

**Skinkpedia uses a source-first research process designed to keep species-specific facts attached to the correct animal, measurement and context.**

## Source priority

We generally prioritize:

| Priority | Source type | Typical use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Peer-reviewed research | Taxonomy, physiology, ecology, behavior, biomechanics |
| 2 | Veterinary guidance | Health, safety, husbandry risks |
| 3 | Universities / academic institutions | Natural history, taxonomy, care research |
| 4 | Zoos and government wildlife agencies | Species profiles, distribution, conservation, legal status |
| 5 | Conservation organizations | Threat status, habitat and population context |
| 6 | Specialist husbandry sources | Captive setup where primary literature is sparse |
| 7 | Commercial sources | Product specifications and dated market examples |
| 8 | Community reports | Anecdotal context only, clearly labeled |

## Entity-source matching

A source is not considered sufficient merely because it discusses “a skink.”

Before transferring a factual value, we check:

**source → species → attribute → measurement → value**

For example:

- a humidity recommendation for an Emerald Tree Skink should not automatically be used for a Sandfish Skink;
- a 140°F desert basking **surface** should not be rewritten as 140°F ambient air;
- a snout-to-vent length should not be labeled as total length;
- a group-housing observation in the wild should not automatically become a captive cohabitation recommendation.

## Citation display

Skinkpedia keeps source accountability visible while avoiding reference sections that become outbound-link blocks.

Article reference sections may list source names, authors, journal names, agencies, publication years, titles and DOI numbers as plain bibliographic text. Live external source links are reserved for trust, profile, contact, policy or methodology pages when they help readers verify the site, contributor background or editorial process.

## Taxonomy

Accepted scientific names are checked against current taxonomic sources where taxonomy affects identification or page meaning.

Older synonyms may still be included when they help readers interpret older literature.

## Care guidance

For captive care, we look for direct species-specific information on:

- enclosure dimensions;
- substrate;
- heating;
- UVB;
- humidity;
- diet;
- water;
- social housing;
- lifespan.

When direct evidence is weak or sources disagree, Skinkpedia should explain the uncertainty or avoid false precision.

## Current and time-sensitive information

The following require periodic rechecking:

- protected status;
- wildlife regulations;
- scientific names;
- breeder/retailer prices;
- product availability;
- veterinary recommendations;
- conservation assessments.

A price table is a dated snapshot, not a permanent market average.

## Review and update signals

Main article pages display named writer/reviewer links and visible published/updated dates near the title. Structured data repeats the same author, reviewer and date information so search systems and assistive tools can read it consistently.

Update dates are changed when the page receives a meaningful factual, structural, source or review update. Minor formatting-only changes do not require rewriting the article’s factual position.

## Internal linking and subject boundaries

Skinkpedia's articles form a structured topic network.

General pages explain universal concepts and route readers to species pages for exact values. Regional pages summarize species occurrence and route deeper biology to dedicated species profiles. Controlled off-topic word-sense pages are kept separate from husbandry navigation.

This helps each URL answer one primary user task without repeating the entire site.
