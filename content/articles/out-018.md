# Skinks in Texas: Species, Identification, Habitat & Safety

**Texas has eight native skink species: the Coal Skink, Common Five-lined Skink, Broad-headed Skink, Many-lined Skink, Great Plains Skink, Prairie Skink, Four-lined Skink, and Little Brown/Ground Skink.** Texas spans pine forest, prairie, limestone country, thorn-scrub, and dry western landscapes, so the most likely species depends strongly on where in the state the animal was found.

For the broader U.S. and Canadian regional network, see the [North American skink directory](/wild-skinks/north-america/).

## Which skink species are native to Texas?

**Eight native species are represented in Texas field-guide and checklist sources.**

| Species | Scientific name | Main identification clue | Texas context |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Coal Skink** | *Plestiodon anthracinus* | Dark lateral band bordered by pale lines | Primarily wetter eastern habitats |
| **Common Five-lined Skink** | *Plestiodon fasciatus* | Five pale stripes; blue juvenile tail | East Texas and wooded areas |
| **Broad-headed Skink** | *Plestiodon laticeps* | Large; breeding male has broad orange-red head | Eastern wooded Texas |
| **Many-lined Skink** | *Plestiodon multivirgatus* | Striped plains/western skink | Northern and western Texas records |
| **Great Plains Skink** | *Plestiodon obsoletus* | Very large; dark-edged scales create an oblique or mosaic pattern | Most of Texas except much of the eastern third |
| **Prairie Skink** | *Plestiodon septentrionalis* | Medium grassland/prairie skink | Northern and prairie regions |
| **Four-lined Skink** | *Plestiodon tetragrammus* | Broad dark side bands bordered by pale lines | Western, central, and southern Texas |
| **Little Brown / Ground Skink** | *Scincella lateralis* | Tiny bronze-brown ground-layer skink | Mainly eastern and other suitable mesic habitats |

## How do you identify a Texas skink?

**Start with region and adult size, then compare stripes, side bands, scale pattern, and life stage.**

Use this sequence:

1. **Region:** East, North/Panhandle, Central/Edwards, South, or West Texas.
2. **Adult size:** Great Plains Skinks are much larger than Ground Skinks.
3. **Body pattern:** five-lined, lateral-band, dark-edged scales, or small uniform-brown form.
4. **Life stage:** juvenile *Plestiodon* can be much darker and more colorful than adults.
5. **Tail color:** blue is useful but not species-specific.
6. **Scale characters:** use a field guide when similar species overlap.

### Great Plains Skink

**The Great Plains Skink (*Plestiodon obsoletus*) is the largest skink species in Texas.**

Texas field references give an adult total length of roughly **6.5–13.5 inches (16–34 cm)**.

Typical traits include:

- gray, tan, or olive-brown adult coloration;
- dark margins on the scales that create an oblique or mosaic appearance;
- a heavy body;
- burrowing behavior;
- juveniles that can be very dark with bright blue tails.

### Four-lined Skink

**The Four-lined Skink (*Plestiodon tetragrammus*) is a medium-sized species strongly associated with southern, central, and western Texas.**

Texas field references place adults around **5–7 inches (12.5–17.5 cm)** total length.

The strongest visual character is a broad dark band on each side bordered by pale lines. The exact band length and pattern vary between the Texas subspecies.

### East Texas lookalikes

In East Texas, a striped or blue-tailed skink may be:

- Common Five-lined;
- Broad-headed;
- Coal;
- or another local *Plestiodon* depending on age and habitat.

Adult male Broad-headed Skinks become much easier to recognize because of their size and broad orange-red head.

## Where in Texas are skinks found?

**The species set changes across Texas ecoregions.**

| Texas region | Representative skinks | Habitat emphasis |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **East Texas / Piney Woods** | Common Five-lined, Broad-headed, Coal, Ground | Woodland, forest edge, leaf litter, logs, rocks, shaded cover |
| **North Texas / Panhandle / prairie** | Great Plains, Prairie, Many-lined | Grassland, prairie, loose soil, rocks, burrows, open ground with cover |
| **Central Texas / Edwards Plateau** | Great Plains, Four-lined | Limestone, rocky slopes, grassland, scrub, burrowable soil |
| **South Texas / Rio Grande region** | Four-lined, with other species locally | Thorn-scrub, sandy or rocky ground, brush, coastal-plain habitats |
| **West Texas** | Great Plains, Many-lined, Four-lined in suitable areas | Semi-arid grassland, rocky terrain, scrub, crevices and burrows |

These are broad regional associations, not guarantees for every county.

## What is the black skink in Texas?

**A “black skink” in Texas is often a juvenile *Plestiodon* rather than a uniformly black adult species.**

Juvenile Great Plains Skinks and several other *Plestiodon* can have:

- very dark bodies;
- bright blue tails;
- contrasting pale marks or spots.

Use body size, stripe pattern, region, and habitat to narrow the identification.

## What is the blue-tailed skink in Texas?

**Several juvenile Texas skinks can have blue tails, so “blue-tailed skink” is not one Texas species.**

A blue-tailed juvenile may belong to:

- Common Five-lined;
- Broad-headed;
- Great Plains;
- or another regional *Plestiodon*.

For common-name disambiguation, see the [Blue-Tailed Skink Guide](/wild-skinks/blue-tailed-skink/).

## Are Texas skinks poisonous?

**Texas skinks are not considered venomous based on current evidence.** The rural name “scorpion lizard” reflects folklore rather than a demonstrated venom system.

A large Great Plains or Broad-headed Skink can still bite if restrained. That is a mechanical injury, not envenomation.

For bite and pet-ingestion details, see [Are Skinks Poisonous or Dangerous?](/skinks/are-skinks-poisonous-dangerous/).

## Are Texas skinks dangerous to dogs or cats?

**A pet is not expected to suffer skink-specific venom poisoning, but eating wild prey can still cause problems.**

Possible concerns include:

- vomiting;
- gastrointestinal irritation;
- parasite exposure;
- choking or trauma.

Discourage pets from hunting native reptiles.

## What do Texas skinks eat?

**Texas skinks primarily eat small animal prey, especially invertebrates, although the exact diet differs by species.**

Common prey categories include insects, spiders, larvae, and other small arthropods. Large Great Plains Skinks can take larger prey than small Ground Skinks.

## Frequently asked questions

### How many native skink species live in Texas?

Eight native species are represented in current Texas field-guide/checklist sources.

### What is the largest skink in Texas?

The Great Plains Skink, reaching roughly 6.5–13.5 inches total length in Texas field references.

### What is a black skink with a blue tail in Texas?

Often a juvenile *Plestiodon*, potentially a Great Plains Skink or another regional species. Location and pattern are needed.

### What is the blue-tailed skink in Texas?

Several juvenile *Plestiodon* species can have blue tails, so it is not one species name.

### Is a “scorpion lizard” in Texas venomous?

No evidence supports Texas skinks as venomous. The name is folklore.

### What skinks live in East Texas?

Common Five-lined, Broad-headed, Coal, and Little Brown/Ground Skinks are important eastern examples.

### What skink lives in South Texas?

The Four-lined Skink is a major South Texas species and also occurs in parts of central and western Texas.

### Are Texas skinks poisonous to dogs?

They are not considered venomous. Dogs that eat wild lizards can still develop digestive or parasite-related problems.
