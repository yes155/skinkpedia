# North American Skinks: Species & Identification by State and Region

**North America contains many native skinks, but this Skinkpedia directory is intentionally focused on the United States and Canada because the mapped search demand is primarily state- and region-based.** Identification works best by combining **location → body pattern → scale characters → size → life stage**.

Use this page to narrow a skink by region first, then move to the relevant species or state guide for finer identification.

## What kinds of skinks live in the United States and Canada?

The most familiar native groups include:
- ***Plestiodon*** — five-lined, Broad-headed, Western, prairie, Great Plains, coal, Gilbert’s, mole and related skinks;
- ***Scincella*** — small ground skinks such as *Scincella lateralis*.

## Why are some North American skinks blue?

In U.S. and Canadian searches, “blue skink” often refers to juvenile *Plestiodon* with bright blue tails, not to one species called the Blue Skink.

Examples include juvenile:
- [Common Five-lined Skinks](/wild-skinks/five-lined-skink/)
- [Broad-headed Skinks](/wild-skinks/broad-headed-skink/)
- [Western Skinks](/wild-skinks/western-skink/)

For common-name disambiguation, see [Blue-Tailed Skink Guide](/wild-skinks/blue-tailed-skink/).

## Midwestern and Great Plains skinks

Important taxa include Prairie Skinks, Great Plains Skink, Common Five-lined Skink in parts of the Midwest, and Coal Skink in parts of its range.

## Western skinks

Key examples include:
- [Western Skink](/wild-skinks/western-skink/)
- Gilbert’s Skink
- other regionally restricted western *Plestiodon*

For state-level detail, see [Skinks in California](/wild-skinks/california/).

## Florida specialty skinks

Florida contains widespread southeastern skinks plus highly specialized sand-ridge species, including the [Florida Sand Skink](/wild-skinks/florida-sand-skink/) and mole-skink forms.

## Are alligator lizards skinks?

No. “Alligator skink” is a common search confusion. Western alligator lizards belong to Anguidae, not Scincidae. See the [Alligator Skink / Alligator Lizard guide](/wild-skinks/alligator-skink/).

## How to identify a skink by state

1. Start with location.
2. Check tail and stripe pattern.
3. Compare adult size.
4. Use scale characters when needed.
5. Do not rely on color alone.
6. Confirm with a state or provincial wildlife source.

## Frequently asked questions
### What skinks live in North America?
Many species occur across the continent. This directory focuses on U.S. and Canadian taxa, especially *Plestiodon* and *Scincella*.

### What is the blue skink in my yard?
In the U.S. and Canada, a bright blue tail often indicates a juvenile *Plestiodon*. Location is needed to narrow the species.

### What skinks live in California?
Western Skinks and Gilbert’s Skinks are prominent examples. See [Skinks in California](/wild-skinks/california/).

### What skinks live in Florida?
Florida has widespread southeastern skinks plus sand-ridge specialists. See [Skinks in Florida](/wild-skinks/florida/).

### Are there skinks in Canada?
Yes. Common Five-lined Skinks occur in Ontario and Western Skinks reach British Columbia.

### What is the largest native U.S. skink?
The Great Plains Skink is among the largest, reaching roughly 13 inches or more in common field references.

### Are North American skinks poisonous?
Current venom research does not support Scincidae as a venomous lizard family. See [Are Skinks Poisonous or Dangerous?](/skinks/are-skinks-poisonous-dangerous/).
