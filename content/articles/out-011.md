# How to Keep Skinks Out of the House: Humane Exclusion & Deterrents

**The most reliable way to keep skinks out of a house is physical exclusion: close entry gaps, reduce insect activity around doors and windows, and remove indoor animals without harming them.** Scent repellents, essential oils, mothballs, and improvised chemical deterrents are not a substitute for sealing access points.

This guide follows **entry points → exclusion → humane removal → prevention**. For backyard coexistence, use the [garden skinks guide](/backyard-skinks/garden-skinks/).

## Why do skinks enter houses?

**Most indoor skink encounters are accidental.** A skink may follow insects through an opening, move into a sheltered space during hot, cold, or dry weather, or enter through a door, vent, window gap, or utility penetration.

Skinks do not establish mammal-like household infestations, and they do not need to be poisoned or exterminated.

Common entry areas include:

- gaps beneath exterior doors;
- damaged screens;
- openings around pipes or cables;
- poorly fitted vents;
- cracks around foundations or wall penetrations;
- open garage or patio doors.

## How to keep skinks from getting inside

### 1. Seal exterior gaps

Inspect the lowest parts of the building first because many skinks travel close to the ground.

Useful repairs include:

- door sweeps and weather stripping;
- repair of torn window and patio screens;
- caulk or appropriate exterior sealant around small cracks and service penetrations;
- correctly fitted covers or mesh where ventilation openings require screening.

Do **not** block required drainage or ventilation features such as weep holes without checking the building design. If screening is appropriate, use a material and opening size that maintains the function of the vent.

### 2. Reduce prey near entry points

Exterior lights can attract flying insects, which in turn can attract insect-eating lizards.

Where practical:

- switch off unnecessary lights near doors;
- use lighting only where needed;
- keep doors closed when lights are attracting insects;
- repair insect-entry gaps at the same time as lizard-entry gaps.

### 3. Remove shelter directly against the foundation

You do not need to strip the whole yard of wildlife habitat. Instead, keep the immediate building perimeter easier to inspect.

Consider moving:

- stacked boards;
- dense piles of leaves;
- loose debris;
- firewood;
- flat objects used as hiding cover

away from doors, vents, and repeated entry points.

There is no evidence-based universal requirement for a **2–3 foot vegetation-free strip**, so that fixed measurement has been removed.

### 4. Fix persistent moisture problems

Repair leaks or standing water near foundations where these conditions are also attracting insects or creating sheltered microhabitats.

The goal is building maintenance, not making the entire yard dry or hostile to wildlife.

## How to safely remove a skink from the house

**For a single visible skink, a container-and-card method is usually the simplest humane option.**

1. Close nearby interior doors so the skink stays in one room.
2. Move pets and children away.
3. Place a lightweight plastic container or box over the skink.
4. Slide stiff cardboard underneath.
5. Carry the covered container outdoors.
6. Release the skink into sheltered vegetation or ground cover away from the doorway.

Do not grab the animal by the tail. Many skinks can drop the tail as a defense.

For tail biology, see [why skinks drop their tails](/skink-biology/tail-drop-regrowth/).

## Should you use a skink trap?

**A trap is rarely necessary for one visible indoor skink.** The container method avoids prolonged confinement and lets you release the animal immediately.

If a live-capture trap is used:

- it must not use adhesive;
- it should be checked frequently;
- it should protect the animal from overheating;
- it should allow prompt release.

Avoid glue boards for lizards. Adhesive traps can injure skin and limbs and can cause prolonged distress during escape attempts.

## Do skink repellents work?

**There is little good evidence that a household scent reliably keeps skinks away.** Search results commonly recommend garlic, peppermint, clove, cinnamon, ammonia, or essential oils, but those suggestions should not be presented as proven skink repellents.

Physical exclusion is more reliable because it removes the route of entry rather than depending on an animal’s response to a smell.

## What smells do skinks hate?

There is no well-established, species-wide “skink-repellent smell” supported by strong evidence.

Avoid spraying concentrated essential oils around reptiles, pets, children, or food areas simply because an online source claims that lizards dislike the odor.

## Should you use mothballs?

**No. Do not use mothballs as a skink repellent around foundations, crawlspaces, gardens, or open rooms unless the product label specifically permits that use.**

In the United States, EPA guidance states that pesticide products must be used according to their label. Mothballs are intended for specific enclosed-storage uses, not as a general wildlife repellent.

## How to prevent repeat entry

After removing the animal:

- inspect the room and exterior wall for openings;
- repair the likely entry point;
- check door sweeps;
- repair screens;
- reduce insects around the doorway;
- move hiding debris away from the exact problem area.

If you repeatedly find droppings indoors, use [What Does Skink Poop Look Like?](/backyard-skinks/skink-poop-feces/) to help determine whether a lizard is actually responsible.

## Frequently asked questions
### Why is there a skink in my house?

It probably entered accidentally through an opening while following prey or seeking shelter.

### What is the best skink repellent?

Physical exclusion is more dependable than a scent repellent. Seal entry gaps and reduce insect activity around doors and windows.

### What smells do skinks hate?

No specific household odor has strong evidence as a reliable skink repellent.

### Can I use peppermint oil or garlic to repel skinks?

Do not rely on them. Their effectiveness is poorly established, and concentrated oils can create unnecessary exposure risks for pets and other animals.

### Do mothballs keep skinks away?

They should not be used as a general lizard repellent. Follow the pesticide label; outdoor or open-area wildlife use may be inconsistent with the registered use.

### What is the safest way to catch a skink indoors?

Cover it with a lightweight container, slide cardboard underneath, and release it outdoors.

### Should I use glue traps for skinks?

No. Adhesive traps can injure reptiles and are unnecessary when humane capture and exclusion are available.

### How do I stop skinks coming back?

Find and seal the entry point, maintain door sweeps and screens, reduce insects near entrances, and remove hiding debris from the immediate foundation area.
