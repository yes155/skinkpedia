# Florida Sand Skink Guide: Identification, Habitat, Range & Conservation

**The Florida Sand Skink (*Plestiodon reynoldsi*) is a small, highly specialized burrowing skink found only in central Florida’s ancient sandy ridges.** It has extremely reduced limbs, a wedge-shaped head, tiny eyes, no external ear openings, and moves through loose sand with body undulations often described as “sand swimming.”

As of September 2026, the species remains **federally listed as Threatened** under the U.S. Endangered Species Act and Florida lists it as **Federally-designated Threatened**.

## How do you identify a Florida Sand Skink?

FWC describes a smooth, elongated lizard reaching about **5 inches (12.7 cm)** total length, with:
- one toe on each front limb;
- two toes on each hind limb;
- small eyes;
- no external ear openings;
- a wedge-shaped snout.

## Why are its legs so small?

The reduced limbs are part of a body plan specialized for movement beneath loose sand. Available research also records approximately **41–42 pre-sacral vertebrae**, consistent with its unusually elongated trunk.

## How does it “swim” through sand?

It moves below the surface using lateral body undulations while keeping its tiny limbs close to the body. Wildlife surveys often detect the species by the characteristic wavy tracks it leaves in sand.

## Where do Florida Sand Skinks live?

FWC currently records the species in **seven central Florida counties**:
- Highlands
- Lake
- Marion
- Orange
- Osceola
- Polk
- Putnam

Habitats include rosemary scrub, scrubby flatwoods, sand pine and oak scrub, turkey oak ridge and related open xeric sandy habitat.

For the broader regional layer, see [Skinks in Florida](/wild-skinks/florida/).

## What do Florida Sand Skinks eat?

FWC lists small invertebrate prey including:
- antlions;
- spiders;
- beetle larvae;
- termites.

## Florida Sand Skink vs. blue-tailed mole skink

The Florida Sand Skink is far more elongated and has dramatically reduced limbs. Blue-tailed mole-skink forms retain more functional limbs and may show conspicuous blue tail coloration.

## Is the Florida Sand Skink endangered?

Habitat loss and fragmentation remain major conservation concerns.

## Can you collect one?

Do not collect, handle or disturb a Florida Sand Skink for a pet. It is protected under federal and state conservation law, and activities that may harm or take listed animals can require authorization.

## Frequently asked questions
### How big does a Florida Sand Skink get?
About 5 inches total length according to FWC.

### Is it endangered or threatened?
It is federally **Threatened**.

### Where is it found?
In central Florida sand-ridge habitats, including Highlands, Lake, Marion, Orange, Osceola, Polk and Putnam counties.

### Why does it have tiny legs?
Its extreme limb reduction is associated with a fossorial, sand-swimming body plan.

### What does it eat?
Small invertebrates such as antlions, spiders, beetle larvae and termites.

### Can I keep one as a pet?
Do not collect one from the wild; it is a protected threatened species.

### Is it the same as the pet Sandfish Skink?
No. Florida Sand Skink is *Plestiodon reynoldsi*; Sandfish Skink is *Scincus scincus* from North Africa and southwest Asia.
