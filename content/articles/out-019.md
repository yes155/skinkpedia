# Skinks in North Carolina: Species, Identification, Habitat & Range

**North Carolina has five native skink species: the Common Five-lined Skink, Southeastern Five-lined Skink, Broad-headed Skink, Coal Skink, and Ground Skink.** The first three are easiest to confuse as juveniles because they can share dark bodies, pale stripes, and bright blue tails. Region, adult size, and subcaudal scale pattern are therefore more useful than tail color alone.

For the broader regional network, see the [North American skink directory](/wild-skinks/north-america/).

## Which skinks live in North Carolina?

**Five native skink species are recognized by North Carolina wildlife references.**

| Species | Scientific name | Main identification clue | North Carolina context |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Common Five-lined Skink** | *Plestiodon fasciatus* | Five pale stripes, blue juvenile tail; enlarged median subcaudal row | Common through much of the state, especially Piedmont and inner Coastal Plain |
| **Southeastern Five-lined Skink** | *Plestiodon inexpectatus* | Similar striped juvenile; subcaudal scales more uniform | Drier sandy habitats, especially Coastal Plain |
| **Broad-headed Skink** | *Plestiodon laticeps* | Largest NC skink; breeding males with broad orange-red heads | Lowland and mid-elevation wooded habitat |
| **Coal Skink** | *Plestiodon anthracinus* | Broad dark lateral band with pale borders | Mainly higher-elevation cool, moist mountain habitat |
| **Ground Skink** | *Scincella lateralis* | Tiny bronze-brown leaf-litter skink | Coastal Plain and eastern Piedmont, with some mountain records |

## How do you identify North Carolina's five-lined-type skinks?

**Common Five-lined, Southeastern Five-lined, and Broad-headed Skinks are hardest to separate when young.**

### Common Five-lined Skink

Typical traits include:

- five pale juvenile stripes;
- bright blue juvenile tail;
- females often retaining more striping as adults;
- adult males becoming more bronze with reddish-orange head color;
- an **enlarged middle row of scales beneath the tail**.

For deeper species biology, see the [Five-lined Skink Guide](/wild-skinks/five-lined-skink/).

### Southeastern Five-lined Skink

North Carolina wildlife references place this species around **5.5–8.5 inches** total length.

Useful traits include:

- five juvenile stripes;
- vivid blue juvenile tail;
- adults becoming browner or olive;
- **subcaudal scales that remain roughly equal in size rather than forming an enlarged median row**.

It is especially associated with drier, sandy environments such as pine-oak sandhills and maritime forests.

### Broad-headed Skink

**The Broad-headed Skink is the largest skink in North Carolina.**

North Carolina wildlife references give roughly **6.5–12.75 inches** total length.

Mature males develop:

- a broad head;
- orange-to-red breeding coloration;
- a larger, heavier body than the five-lined species.

The species is semi-arboreal and uses trees, loose bark, cavities, logs, and wooded habitat.

For species-level depth, see the [Broad-headed Skink Guide](/wild-skinks/broad-headed-skink/).

## Where in North Carolina are skinks found?

**The state's Blue Ridge, Piedmont, and Coastal Plain support different skink assemblages.**

| North Carolina region | Representative skinks | Habitat emphasis |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Blue Ridge / mountains** | Coal Skink; Common Five-lined at suitable lower and mid elevations; occasional Ground Skink records | Cool moist slopes, rocky stream valleys, seepages, woodland cover |
| **Piedmont** | Common Five-lined, Broad-headed, Ground, with Southeastern Five-lined in suitable areas | Hardwood/mixed forest, logs, leaf litter, tree cover, disturbed edges |
| **Coastal Plain** | Southeastern Five-lined, Broad-headed, Ground, and Common Five-lined in parts of the inner Coastal Plain | Sandy pine-oak habitat, maritime forest, swamp forest, leaf litter and woody cover |

This regional table is a field-orientation tool rather than a county-level range map.

## Where do Common Five-lined Skinks live in North Carolina?

**Common Five-lined Skinks occur through much of North Carolina but not every habitat or county.**

They are especially associated with:

- Piedmont;
- inner Coastal Plain;
- lower and mid-elevation wooded areas;
- logs, rocks, bark, leaf litter, and similar cover.

They are absent from the highest mountain elevations and from some Coastal Plain localities.

## Where do Coal Skinks live in North Carolina?

**Coal Skinks are primarily mountain skinks in North Carolina.**

They favor:

- cool, moist hardwood forest;
- north-facing slopes;
- springs and seepages;
- rocky stream valleys;
- bluff edges.

North Carolina wildlife references place adults around **5–7 inches** total length.

Identification traits include a broad dark lateral stripe bordered by paler lines and a pattern that differs from the five-lined species.

## Where do Ground Skinks live in North Carolina?

**Ground Skinks are tiny leaf-litter specialists, most common in the Coastal Plain and eastern Piedmont.**

North Carolina wildlife references give roughly **3–5.75 inches** total length.

Typical traits include:

- smooth, slender body;
- brown or bronze coloration;
- dark lateral stripe;
- very small limbs;
- rapid, snake-like movement through leaves.

For deeper species coverage, see the [Little Brown/Ground Skink Guide](/wild-skinks/little-brown-ground-skink/).

## What is the blue-tailed skink in North Carolina?

**A blue-tailed juvenile is most likely a Common Five-lined, Southeastern Five-lined, or Broad-headed Skink.**

Because juveniles overlap strongly in color, use:

1. locality;
2. adult size if known;
3. subcaudal scales;
4. other head/scale characters;
5. habitat.

For the broader name confusion, see the [Blue-Tailed Skink Guide](/wild-skinks/blue-tailed-skink/).

## What do North Carolina skinks eat?

**All five native species feed heavily on invertebrates, but prey size and hunting habitat differ.**

Common prey can include:

- insects;
- spiders;
- larvae;
- earthworms;
- isopods and other small invertebrates.

A Broad-headed Skink can take larger prey than the much smaller Ground Skink.

## Should you handle a wild North Carolina skink?

**Usually, no.** Handling is unnecessary for most identification and can cause defensive biting or tail loss.

Observation and photography are normally sufficient. North Carolina also regulates collection of nongame wildlife, so current rules should be checked before capturing native reptiles.

## Frequently asked questions

### How many native skink species live in North Carolina?

Five: Common Five-lined, Southeastern Five-lined, Broad-headed, Coal, and Ground Skinks.

### What is the largest skink in North Carolina?

The Broad-headed Skink, at roughly 6.5–12.75 inches total length in North Carolina wildlife guidance.

### What is the smallest skink in North Carolina?

The Ground Skink, at roughly 3–5.75 inches total length.

### How do you tell Common and Southeastern Five-lined Skinks apart?

Common Five-lined Skinks have an enlarged middle row of subcaudal scales; Southeastern Five-lined Skinks have more uniformly sized subcaudal scales.

### What is the blue-tailed skink in North Carolina?

Usually a juvenile Common Five-lined, Southeastern Five-lined, or Broad-headed Skink.

### Where are Coal Skinks found in North Carolina?

Mainly in cool, moist mountain habitats such as rocky slopes, springs, seepages, and stream valleys.

### Do Ground Skinks live in North Carolina?

Yes. They are North Carolina's smallest native lizards and occur mainly in the Coastal Plain and eastern Piedmont.

### Are North Carolina skinks poisonous?

The state's native skinks are not considered venomous. Broader dog, cat, and bite questions belong in the [skink safety guide](/skinks/are-skinks-poisonous-dangerous/).
