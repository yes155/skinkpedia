# Gidgee Skink Guide: Habitat, Social Behavior, Size & Captive-Care Context

**The Gidgee Skink (*Egernia stokesii*) is a robust Australian skink known for rock-crevice habitat, stable social groups, and long-term associations among related individuals.** Compare its group-living biology with other captive species in the [Pet Skinks guide](/pet-skinks/). The most useful captive-care context comes from that biology: secure crevices, safe basking access, enough resources to reduce competition, and a cautious approach to group housing.

This is a species guide rather than a full husbandry prescription, so exact equipment and climate values should be taken from a current specialist care plan for the keeper's locality and enclosure.

## Size and appearance

Gidgee Skinks are robust, short-tailed skinks adapted to wedging into crevices.

Published subspecies and population data vary in adult size. Many captive-care references place typical animals in roughly the **7–10 inch (18–25 cm)** total-length range, while some subspecies grow larger.

The tail is heavily keeled and spiny and helps the skink brace itself inside refuge sites.

## Rock-crevice habitat

**Gidgee Skinks are strongly associated with stable rock or log crevices.**

Field studies in South Australia found animals repeatedly using shared crevice refuges on rocky outcrops. Crevice availability is closely tied to how social groups are organized.

Captive implications include:

- provide multiple secure crevices;
- anchor heavy rocks so they cannot collapse;
- offer more than one basking and feeding site in group housing;
- maintain visual barriers and escape routes;
- avoid decorative rock stacks that can be undermined.

## Social behavior

**Gidgee Skinks are among the best-studied group-living lizards.**

A six-season field study documented **17 stable social groups of 2–17 individuals**, with most juveniles remaining in their parents' social group for years. Other studies have found persistent group structure across multiple rocky outcrops.

This wild sociality does **not** mean unfamiliar captive adults can automatically be housed together.

If keeping more than one:

- start with animals of known compatibility where possible;
- duplicate basking, feeding, and refuge resources;
- watch for chasing, biting, exclusion, or weight loss;
- keep a separate enclosure ready.

## Captive enclosure context

A captive enclosure should emphasize **usable horizontal space plus stable crevice structure**, not empty volume.

For a pair or small established group, current specialist keepers commonly use enclosures around **4 × 2 × 2 ft (120 × 60 × 60 cm) or larger**, but the more important requirement is sufficient resources and separation opportunities for the actual animals.

## Heat, light and humidity context

Published research confirms that Gidgee Skinks are diurnal basking lizards from arid to semi-arid Australian habitats.

A practical enclosure should provide:

- a warm basking area;
- a cooler retreat;
- bright visible daytime light;
- appropriate UVB;
- dry-to-moderate background humidity;
- clean drinking water.

Because robust species-specific captive UVI and climate standards are less well established than for blue-tongue or ocellated skinks, avoid treating one breeder's exact temperature or UVI as a universal biological requirement.

## Diet

**Gidgee Skinks are omnivorous with a substantial plant component.**

Published reviews of *Egernia* ecology describe larger species as increasingly herbivorous while still consuming invertebrates.

A captive feeding plan should use:

- varied leafy and plant material;
- appropriate invertebrate foods;
- species-specific supplementation;
- portion control based on body condition.

## Frequently asked questions

### Are Gidgee Skinks social?

Yes. Field studies document stable groups of related individuals sharing refuges for years.

### Can Gidgee Skinks live together in captivity?

Compatible animals sometimes can, but wild sociality is not a guarantee of captive compatibility. Group housing requires duplicate resources, monitoring, and immediate separation capacity.

### Where do Gidgee Skinks live?

They are Australian skinks strongly associated with rocky outcrops, crevices, and in some populations hollow logs or woody refuge structures.

### What should a Gidgee Skink enclosure emphasize?

Secure crevices, safe basking access, usable floor area, multiple resources, and stable structures that cannot collapse.

### What do Gidgee Skinks eat?

They are omnivorous, with a strong plant component and some invertebrate prey.
