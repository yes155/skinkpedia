# Monkey-Tailed Skink Guide: Prehensile-Tailed Skink Care, Diet & Enclosure

**The monkey-tailed or Solomon Island prehensile-tailed skink (*Corucia zebrata*) is a large, arboreal, crepuscular herbivorous skink with a fully prehensile tail.** Its care differs sharply from terrestrial skinks because it needs substantial vertical structure, plant-based feeding, and careful management of social interactions.

For general principles, see the [skink care guide](/skink-care/) and [skink diet guide](/skink-care/diet/). If you are still comparing species before choosing one, use the [Pet Skinks guide](/pet-skinks/).

## Size, tail and natural history

*Corucia zebrata* is among the largest living skinks. Comparative morphology data report an average SVL of about **275 mm**, while species husbandry references describe adults reaching roughly **24–32 inches total length**.

The prehensile tail functions as an additional gripping structure while the skink moves and feeds among branches. The species is native to Solomon Islands rainforest and is primarily active around dawn and dusk.

Captive-husbandry references place longevity at approximately **15–20 years**, making this a long-term commitment.

## Enclosure size and climbing structure

**A Solomon Island prehensile-tailed skink care sheet recommends a custom enclosure and states that 4 ft long × 2 ft wide × 4 ft high is the minimum for a pair.** It also notes that a single skink can be housed in a suitably modified 55-gallon enclosure.

For modern welfare planning, the key point is not to compress a large arboreal skink into minimal space. The enclosure should provide:

- strong branches;
- elevated hiding sites;
- secure climbing routes;
- multiple resting heights;
- safe access to heat and light;
- enough room for the animal to turn and climb without crowding.

All heavy branches and platforms must support the skink’s full weight.

## Temperature and lighting

The species-specific care sheet gives:

- **daytime temperature: 80–90°F (27–32°C);**
- **basking area: up to 95°F (35°C).**

A gradient is important so the animal can choose its preferred temperature.

The older species care sheet recommends full-spectrum ultraviolet lighting. Modern UV guidance is better expressed as a measured Ferguson-zone gradient rather than by bulb percentage alone.

Baines et al.'s UV-Tool places *Corucia zebrata* in **Ferguson Zone 2**. In practical terms, provide a measurable low-to-moderate UV gradient on accessible upper branches, with deep shade immediately available. Ferguson Zone 2 corresponds to typical habitat exposure around UVI 0.7–1.0, with higher short-term exposure possible within the zone; the enclosure should offer choice rather than one fixed UV level everywhere.

## Diet

**Prehensile-tailed skinks are herbivorous.** The species care sheet recommends:

- **50–60% leafy greens;**
- **30–40% mixed vegetables and sliced sweet potato;**
- **less than 5% fruit and similar extras.**

Examples of greens in the source include collard, mustard, turnip, and dandelion greens.

Avoid feeding routines that displace the species' plant-based diet with animal protein.

## Calcium and supplements

The older species care sheet recommends routine calcium supplementation, but exact frequency should be interpreted cautiously because product formulations, UVB provision, age, and veterinary guidance vary.

Use species-specific product directions and avoid presenting an old supplement schedule as a universal medical prescription.

## Social behavior

**Prehensile-tailed skinks show unusually complex social behavior for lizards, and family-group associations are documented in available research.** The term “circulus” is used in the literature and hobby to describe their social group.

However, natural sociality does not mean unfamiliar adults can be combined casually. Introductions can provoke aggression, and any group must have:

- sufficient space;
- multiple elevated retreats;
- duplicate resources;
- close monitoring;
- immediate separation capacity.

## Handling and safety

These are large arboreal skinks with strong claws and jaws. That does not mean routine handling requires thick leather gloves.

Instead:

- support the body and tail;
- let the animal grip stable surfaces;
- avoid forcing it off branches;
- minimize restraint;
- use protective equipment only when there is a real safety need.

## Frequently asked questions
### Are monkey-tailed skinks herbivores?

Yes. Species-specific care guidance describes a plant-based diet dominated by leafy greens and vegetables.

### What enclosure size does a pair need?

The species-specific care sheet gives 4 × 2 × 4 ft as the minimum for a pair.

### What temperature do they need?

The source gives roughly 80–90°F daytime, with basking spots up to 95°F.

### Do they live in groups?

They can form stable social/family associations, but captive introductions require caution and the ability to separate incompatible animals.
