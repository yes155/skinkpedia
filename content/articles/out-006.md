# Alligator Skink Guide: Identification, Habitat, Range & Natural History

**“Alligator skink” is not a recognized true-skink species name. In North American searches, the term usually refers to an alligator lizard in the family Anguidae, especially *Elgaria*, rather than a skink in the family Scincidae.** The quickest field difference is the body surface: alligator lizards have rougher, keeled scales and a distinct lateral fold, while typical skinks have smoother overlapping scales and no comparable side fold.

This guide follows **taxonomic disambiguation → identification → range → ecology**. For true North American skinks, use the [North American skink directory](/wild-skinks/north-america/).

## Is an “alligator skink” actually a skink?

**Usually, no.** Searchers using “alligator skink” are commonly describing an **alligator lizard**, which belongs to **Anguidae**, not Scincidae.

| Trait | Typical true skink | Alligator lizard |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Family | Scincidae | Anguidae |
| Scales | Usually smoother and overlapping | More strongly keeled/armored-looking |
| Body side | No prominent *Elgaria*-type fold | Distinct lateral fold |
| Head | Often streamlined | Relatively large and robust |
| Tail loss | Common in many species | Also capable of autotomy |

## How do you identify an alligator lizard?

Look for a long body, relatively large head, rough-looking scales and the fold of skin running along each side between the dorsal and belly scales. Color varies with species and locality, so geography should support the identification.

## Where do alligator lizards live?

Alligator lizards occur in western North America. Southern Alligator Lizards occur through much of California and into Baja California, while Northern Alligator Lizards extend farther north through the Pacific Northwest into British Columbia.

Habitats can include woodland and forest edges, chaparral, brushy slopes, gardens and suburban cover, and areas beneath logs, boards, rocks and vegetation.

## What do alligator lizards eat?

They are opportunistic predators of invertebrates and small vertebrate prey. Depending on species and size, prey can include spiders, beetles, crickets, grasshoppers, snails, other arthropods and small lizards.

## Do alligator lizards bite?

They can bite defensively if captured or restrained. A large individual can break skin, so wild animals are best observed without handling.

## Frequently asked questions
### Is an alligator skink a real species?
Not as a recognized North American true-skink species. The search term usually refers to an alligator lizard.

### What is the difference between a skink and an alligator lizard?
Alligator lizards have rougher keeled scales and a prominent lateral fold. True skinks belong to Scincidae and generally have smoother overlapping scales.

### Are alligator lizards skinks?
No. Alligator lizards are anguids.

### Where are alligator lizards found?
They occur in western North America, with different *Elgaria* species ranging from Mexico and California north into the Pacific Northwest and British Columbia.

### Do alligator lizards bite?
They can bite when restrained or threatened. Avoid unnecessary handling.

### Can you keep an alligator lizard as a pet?
Collection and possession rules vary by jurisdiction. Do not collect a wild animal without checking local law.
