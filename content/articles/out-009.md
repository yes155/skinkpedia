# Are Skinks Poisonous or Dangerous? Bites, Dogs, Cats & Common Myths

**Current venom research does not support skinks in the family Scincidae as venomous lizards, and there is no established skink-produced poison that explains the common “poisonous blue-tailed skink” or “scorpion lizard” myths.** That does not make every encounter risk-free: skinks can bite defensively, and pets that eat wild lizards can have gastrointestinal problems or acquire parasites.

This guide follows **poison/venom myths → bites → dogs and cats → practical first aid**. For the broader biological context of skinks as a lizard family, see [What Is a Skink?](/skinks/).

## Are skinks poisonous?

There is no good scientific evidence that true skinks produce a poison that makes them toxic to people or pets through ordinary contact.

The safest wording is **“skinks are not considered venomous based on current evidence”**, rather than claiming every encounter is “100% harmless.”

## Are blue-tailed skinks poisonous?

No evidence supports the common North American blue-tailed *Plestiodon* skinks as poisonous or venomous. Their bright juvenile tail is associated with anti-predator signaling, not toxicity.

## Are red-headed or Broad-headed Skinks poisonous?

No. The orange-red breeding coloration of adult male Broad-headed and related *Plestiodon* skinks is not a toxin warning.

## What if a dog eats a skink?

A dog that eats a skink is not expected to be poisoned by a skink-specific toxin, but ingestion of wild prey can cause vomiting, gastrointestinal upset, choking/irritation, parasite exposure or secondary chemical exposure.

Contact a veterinarian for repeated vomiting, marked lethargy, abdominal pain, neurologic signs, breathing difficulty or another significant change.

## What if a cat eats a skink?

Cats are not poisoned by a skink-produced toxin, but eating lizards can expose cats to parasites in some regions.

A key example is **Platynosomum** liver-fluke infection, historically called “lizard poisoning.” Cats acquire it by eating infected lizards or frogs. The illness comes from the parasite, not skink venom.

## What should you do after a skink bite?

1. Avoid jerking the animal away.
2. Once released, wash the wound promptly with **warm soapy water**.
3. Control bleeding with clean pressure if needed.
4. Cover broken skin with a clean dressing.

Seek medical care for a deep or severe wound, uncontrolled bleeding, impaired movement, a bite over a joint, infection signs, higher infection risk, or a tetanus concern.

Do **not** put household disinfectant, reptile enclosure cleaner or hand sanitizer into the wound.

## Are Gila monsters skinks?

No. Gila monsters and beaded lizards belong to Helodermatidae, not Scincidae, and are well-established venomous lizards.

## Frequently asked questions
### Are blue-tailed skinks poisonous to dogs?
There is no evidence of a skink-specific poison. A dog that eats wild prey can still develop digestive or other complications.

### Are blue-tailed skinks poisonous to cats?
They are not known to contain a skink-produced poison. Cats that eat lizards can acquire parasites such as *Platynosomum* in endemic regions.

### Are Broad-headed Skinks poisonous?
No evidence supports them as venomous or poisonous. They can bite defensively.

### Are red-headed skinks poisonous?
No. Red or orange breeding coloration is not evidence of toxicity.

### Are skinks in Florida poisonous?
Florida skinks are not considered venomous based on current Scincidae venom research. Pet ingestion can still create non-venom risks.

### Are skinks in Texas poisonous?
Native skinks are not considered venomous. Correct identification matters because not every lizard in Texas is a skink.

### How do you treat a skink bite?
Wash with warm soapy water and seek medical care for a serious wound or infection signs.
