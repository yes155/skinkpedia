# Crocodile Skink Care: Diet, Handling, Temperature & Daily Husbandry

**Red-eyed crocodile skink care centers on a cool-to-warm thermal range, very high humidity, low-intensity UVB, varied live invertebrates, clean water, dense cover, and minimal unnecessary handling.** The species is a low-light forest-floor skink, so its care values should come from *Tribolonotus gracilis* sources rather than from blue-tongue or desert-skink care sheets.

This guide follows the daily husbandry sequence: care → feeding → handling → routine → health. Physical construction belongs in the [crocodile skink enclosure guide](/pet-skinks/red-eyed-crocodile-skink/enclosure/).

## Crocodile skink care at a glance

The species-specific Reptile Systems care sheet provides the following baseline:

| Care variable | Species-specific guidance |
| --- | --- |
| **Overall temperature range** | **22–32°C (72–89°F)** |
| **Basking/hot spot** | **Maximum 32°C (89°F)** |
| **Typical daytime ambient** | About **28°C (82°F)** |
| **Typical night ambient** | About **22°C (72°F)** |
| **Humidity** | **80–95% relative humidity** |
| **UV exposure** | **Ferguson Zone 1; UVI 0–1.4** |
| **Light cycle** | About **12 hours on / 12 hours off** |
| **Diet** | Insectivorous; varied live invertebrates |
| **Handling** | Tolerates some handling but stresses easily |
| **Experience level** | Moderate |

## Temperature

**Provide a gradient that allows the skink to choose conditions within a roughly 22–32°C range, with the warmest point not exceeding about 32°C in the Reptile Systems framework.**

The care sheet gives:

- hot spot maximum: **32°C**;
- daytime ambient: about **28°C**;
- night ambient: about **22°C**.

This does **not** mean all heat must be switched off at night. If the room would fall below the species’ safe night range, a controlled non-light-emitting heat source may be needed.

Use a thermostat with heating equipment and verify temperatures independently with digital thermometers.

## Humidity and hydration

**Maintain very high humidity while preserving adequate ventilation.** Reptile Systems specifies approximately **80–95% ambient relative humidity** for *T. gracilis*.

Practical humidity management can include:

- daily misting;
- moisture-retentive naturalistic substrate;
- live plants;
- a fresh water bowl;
- adequate but not excessive ventilation;
- digital humidity monitoring.

The goal is a stable humid forest-floor environment, not a sealed, stagnant box.

Fresh water should be provided every day in a small, accessible bowl.

## UVB and lighting

**Red-eyed crocodile skinks are a low-UV species placed in Ferguson Zone 1.** Reptile Systems gives a target range of approximately **UVI 0–1.4**.

The care sheet also recommends a regular day/night cycle of roughly **12 hours light and 12 hours dark**.

Low-intensity UV can be provided with equipment appropriate to Zone 1. The source specifically shows that both a suitable low-output T5 system and a suitable compact UVB lamp can be used, so the previous blanket claim that compact UVB lamps are inherently unsafe or inadequate should be removed.

Always create shaded cover so the animal can leave the light.

## Diet

**Red-eyed crocodile skinks are insectivorous and should receive a varied selection of appropriately sized captive feeder invertebrates.**

The Reptile Systems guide lists foods such as:

- crickets;
- cockroaches;
- locusts;
- silkworms;
- calciworms;
- mealworms as part of a varied diet;
- other commercially produced feeder insects.

Do not describe plant foods as “toxic” merely because this species is insectivorous. The useful husbandry point is that its normal captive diet is based on invertebrates, not vegetables and fruit.

## Gut-loading and supplementation

**Prepare feeder insects nutritionally before feeding rather than relying on one unprepared feeder species.**

Reptile Systems advises gut-loading feeder insects before use. Its adult husbandry guide also suggests, as a general schedule:

- calcium dusting approximately **three times per week**;
- vitamin supplement approximately **twice per week**.

Because supplement formulations differ, these frequencies should be treated as guidance from one specialist husbandry source rather than as a veterinary prescription. Follow the actual product directions and seek reptile-veterinary advice if the animal has a nutritional or health problem.

## Handling and settling in

**Crocodile skinks can tolerate some handling, but unnecessary handling should be kept low because they stress easily.**

The Reptile Systems sheet recommends a **two-week settling-in period** for a newly acquired animal during which handling does not take place.

After acclimation:

- handle only when necessary or when the animal remains calm;
- support the entire body;
- avoid grabbing;
- do not restrain the tail;
- stop if the animal repeatedly struggles, freezes, vocalizes, or attempts to escape.

This is more accurate than describing the species as universally incapable of handling or claiming that ordinary handling causes “stress-induced shock.”

## Daily routine

A simple daily routine is:

1. check temperature and humidity;
2. replace or refresh drinking water;
3. inspect equipment;
4. remove waste and spoiled food;
5. observe breathing, movement, posture, and body condition without repeatedly disturbing hides;
6. mist as required to maintain the target humidity;
7. feed according to the animal’s age, body condition, and established schedule.

Because the species is secretive, monitoring should be efficient and low-disturbance.

## Health warning signs

**Health sections should identify warning signs without diagnosing or treating disease from the page.**

Contact a reptile veterinarian for persistent or serious signs such as:

- labored or open-mouth breathing;
- discharge around the nose or mouth;
- marked weight loss;
- persistent appetite loss;
- wounds, burns, or swelling;
- abnormal movement or weakness;
- severe retained shed with tissue damage;
- rapid deterioration.

Do not prescribe antibiotics, antiparasitic drugs, forced soaking, or treatment-dose supplements from a general care page.

## Frequently asked questions
### What temperature should a crocodile skink enclosure be?

Reptile Systems gives an overall range of about 22–32°C, with a hot spot no higher than about 32°C, daytime ambient around 28°C, and night ambient around 22°C.

### What humidity does a red-eyed crocodile skink need?

The species-specific Reptile Systems care sheet gives approximately 80–95% relative humidity.

### What UVI does a crocodile skink need?

It is a Ferguson Zone 1 species. Reptile Systems recommends a low UVI range of approximately 0–1.4.

### What do red-eyed crocodile skinks eat?

They are insectivorous. Offer a varied rotation of appropriately sized captive feeder invertebrates and gut-load them before feeding.

### Can I handle a crocodile skink?

Some handling is tolerated, but the species stresses easily. Allow a newly acquired animal to settle and keep handling minimal and calm.
