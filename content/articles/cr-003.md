# Crocodile Skink Enclosure: Humidity, Substrate, Water & Hides

This guide follows the enclosure sequence **humidity → substrate → water → cover → ventilation**. Daily feeding and handling belong in the [crocodile skink care guide](/pet-skinks/red-eyed-crocodile-skink/care/).

## Minimum enclosure size

**Reptile Systems recommends a minimum enclosure of 80 × 60 × 60 cm for a single adult red-eyed crocodile skink.**

That metric value is the clearest source figure. The care-sheet graphic prints an inconsistent imperial conversion, so the metric measurement should remain the controlling value rather than repeating the incorrect conversion.

A larger enclosure can be beneficial if it still allows the keeper to maintain:

- high humidity;
- correct temperatures;
- low-light shelter;
- adequate ventilation;
- secure cover;
- reliable monitoring.

## Glass, PVC or other enclosure materials?

Glass, PVC, or another sealed enclosure material can work if the finished habitat maintains the correct environment.

Judge the enclosure by outcome:

- Can it maintain **80–95% humidity**?
- Can it remain adequately ventilated?
- Can it hold the required substrate and furnishings?
- Can heating and UVB be installed safely?
- Is it escape-resistant?

Do not automatically seal 75% of a mesh top. Ventilation and humidity should be balanced by measurement, not by a fixed screen-cover percentage.

## Temperature setup

**Build a gentle gradient within the species-specific Reptile Systems range of approximately 22–32°C.**

Use:

- hot spot: **maximum about 32°C**;
- daytime ambient: about **28°C**;
- night ambient: about **22°C**.

A suitable basking lamp or ceramic heat emitter can be used where the room does not naturally provide the correct range. Regulate heat with a thermostat.

Do not shop by a fixed 40–50 W bulb rule. Wattage depends on enclosure material, room temperature, fixture, and mounting distance.

## UVB setup

**This is a Ferguson Zone 1 species, so UV exposure should remain low.**

Target approximately:

- **UVI 0–1.4**;
- around **12 hours of light and 12 hours of darkness**.

The Reptile Systems sheet gives both a Zone 1 T5 option and a suitable compact 6% UVB option. This means a blanket rule that compact UVB bulbs are always inappropriate is not supported for this species.

Measure the exposure where practical and preserve dense shaded areas.

## Humidity

**Maintain approximately 80–95% relative humidity while retaining enough airflow to prevent the enclosure from becoming stagnant.**

Reptile Systems recommends daily misting and notes that live plants can help maintain humidity.

Useful humidity tools include:

- digital hygrometer;
- moisture-retentive substrate;
- daily manual misting or a controlled misting system;
- live plants;
- water bowl;
- adjustable ventilation.

Automated misting is optional equipment, not a biological requirement.

## Substrate

**Use a naturalistic substrate that holds moisture while allowing normal forest-floor behavior.**

The Reptile Systems care sheet suggests combinations such as:

- soil;
- orchid bark;
- cypress mulch;

## Does a crocodile skink need a paludarium?

**No source used for the corrected care framework requires a water pool covering 30% of the enclosure floor.**

Reptile Systems recommends **fresh water daily in a small bowl** and daily misting to maintain humidity. Natural-history literature does associate *Tribolonotus* with habitat close to water, but that does not establish a mandatory 70/30 land-water split in captivity.

A larger shallow water feature can be used if it is:

- easy to enter and exit;
- hygienic;
- not reducing necessary land area;
- not creating unstable enclosure conditions.

But it should be treated as an optional design choice, not a universal requirement.

## Hides and cover

**Dense cover is more important than an exact number of commercial hides.**

Use features such as:

- cork bark;
- leaf litter;
- low branches;
- pieces of wood;
- naturalistic ground cover;
- secure hide spaces;
- live plants where appropriate.

The goal is to let the skink move through the enclosure without being continuously exposed.

Do not mandate exactly three damp hides unless a species-specific source supports that number.

## Ventilation

**High humidity does not mean eliminating airflow.** Reptile Systems explicitly calls for adequate ventilation.

Monitor the enclosure for:

- stagnant odor;
- persistently waterlogged substrate;
- mold or decomposition beyond the intended naturalistic layer;
- condensation that prevents useful observation;
- humidity that falls outside the target range.

Adjust ventilation and misting together rather than treating them as competing one-way controls.

## Monitoring equipment

At minimum, use:

- digital thermometer(s);
- thermostat for controlled heat;
- digital hygrometer;
- suitable UV monitoring where practical.

Place sensors where the skink actually experiences the environment.

## Setup checklist before introduction

Before the animal arrives:

- [ ] Enclosure is at least 80 × 60 × 60 cm for one adult.
- [ ] Temperature gradient is stable.
- [ ] Hot spot does not exceed the species-specific maximum.
- [ ] Humidity remains around 80–95%.
- [ ] Low-level Zone 1 UVB and shade are available.
- [ ] Naturalistic substrate and dense cover are installed.
- [ ] Fresh water is accessible.
- [ ] Ventilation remains adequate.
- [ ] Heating is thermostatically controlled.
- [ ] The enclosure has been tested through full day/night cycles.

## Frequently asked questions
### What size enclosure does a red-eyed crocodile skink need?

Reptile Systems recommends a minimum of 80 × 60 × 60 cm for a single adult.

### What humidity should the enclosure be?

Approximately 80–95% relative humidity in the Reptile Systems care framework.

### Does a crocodile skink need a 30% water section?

No. The species-specific source used here recommends fresh water in a small bowl and daily misting; it does not mandate a 30% pool.

### Is glass bad for crocodile skinks?

No. Reptile Systems specifically describes a glass enclosure with adequate ventilation as suitable.

### What substrate should I use?

A moisture-retaining naturalistic mix such as soil with orchid bark or cypress mulch, supplemented with leaf litter and decaying wood, is consistent with the Reptile Systems care sheet.
