# Blue-Tongue Skink Care Guide for Beginners

**Blue-tongue skink care requires a secure terrestrial enclosure, a controlled thermogradient, species-appropriate humidity, UVB exposure, clean water, and an omnivorous diet.** Exact humidity and some husbandry details depend on the species or locality, so the first step is to identify which blue-tongue skink you keep.

This guide provides the **care overview**. Physical installation belongs in the [blue-tongue skink enclosure guide](/pet-skinks/blue-tongue-skink/enclosure/), while exact feeding depth belongs in the [blue-tongue skink diet guide](/pet-skinks/blue-tongue-skink/diet/).

## Blue-tongue skink care at a glance

The RSPCA blue-tongue skink care sheet provides the following baseline:

| Care variable | Evidence-based baseline |
| --- | --- |
| **Minimum enclosure** | **120 × 60 × 60 cm** for one blue-tongue skink |
| **Basking zone** | **30–32°C**, up to **35°C for younger skinks** |
| **Cool end** | About **22–26°C** |
| **Night temperature** | About **20–22°C** |
| **UV Index** | **UVI 3.0–5.0** in the basking zone, grading to **0 in shade** |
| **Humidity** | Approximately **40–90% depending on species/locality** |
| **Adult diet** | About **50–60% animal matter, 40–50% vegetables, fruit ≤10%** |
| **Water** | Clean, fresh water available at all times |

These figures should be applied to the exact species/locality rather than treated as proof that every *Tiliqua* needs the same humidity or microclimate.

## Enclosure basics

**A single blue-tongue skink should have a secure, well-ventilated vivarium of at least 120 cm long × 60 cm deep × 60 cm high.** The RSPCA recommends a solid, sealed enclosure that is free from hazards and protected from drafts, direct sunlight, and uncontrolled heat.

The enclosure must be large enough to provide:

- a basking zone;
- a cooler end;
- hiding places;
- a thick burrowing substrate;
- water;
- UVB exposure and shade;
- room for normal terrestrial movement.

For construction materials, heating installation, substrate depth, and lighting placement, see the [blue-tongue skink enclosure guide](/pet-skinks/blue-tongue-skink/enclosure/).

## Temperature and heating

**Blue-tongue skinks need a thermogradient so they can move between warmer and cooler conditions.**

The RSPCA recommends:

- **30–32°C** in the basking zone;
- up to **35°C for younger skinks**;
- approximately **22–26°C** at the cool end;
- approximately **20–22°C at night**.

Use a suitable overhead heat source and regulate it with a thermostat. Verify temperatures with digital thermometers rather than relying on thermostat settings alone.

The exact bulb wattage is not a husbandry target because output changes with enclosure size, room temperature, fixture type, and mounting distance.

## UVB and day/night lighting

**Blue-tongue skinks should have a UVB photogradient rather than one uniform exposure level across the enclosure.**

The RSPCA recommends:

- **UVI 3.0–5.0** in the basking zone;
- a gradient down to **zero in shade**;
- approximately **12 hours of light and 12 hours of darkness** in its example care setup.

UV output should be checked at animal level with an appropriate UV meter where practical, and the animal must be able to leave the illuminated area.

Detailed fixture placement belongs in the [enclosure guide](/pet-skinks/blue-tongue-skink/enclosure/).

## Humidity

**Humidity must match the exact blue-tongue species or locality.** The RSPCA gives a broad range of roughly **40–90% depending on species**, which is deliberately wider than the narrow ranges often repeated in generic care sheets.

Use a hygrometer at the cool end and adjust humidity by balancing:

- ventilation;
- substrate moisture;
- spraying where appropriate;
- local humid refuges;
- room conditions.

Do not assign a Northern, Indonesian, Halmahera, or Merauke humidity target unless the value is supported for that taxon.

## Diet

**Adult blue-tongue skinks are omnivorous.** The RSPCA recommends approximately:

- **50–60% animal matter**;
- **40–50% vegetables**;
- **no more than 10% fruit**.

Suitable foods can include appropriately sized feeder insects, snails from safe captive sources, eggs, selected animal-protein foods, vegetables, and limited fruit.

The exact food list, gut-loading, supplementation, and feeding routine belong in the [blue-tongue skink diet guide](/pet-skinks/blue-tongue-skink/diet/).

## Water and hydration

**Provide a large, shallow dish of clean, fresh water at all times.** The RSPCA recommends placing it in the cool end and replacing the water daily or whenever it is contaminated.

Some skinks may also drink droplets after light spraying, depending on the humidity strategy used for the exact taxon.

## Handling and settling in

**Blue-tongue skinks can tolerate human interaction after they have settled, but newly acquired animals should not be forced into frequent handling.**

The RSPCA recommends:

- setting up and running the vivarium for at least **one week before the animal arrives**;
- allowing the skink to enter the enclosure carefully;
- keeping interaction minimal on the first day;
- avoiding lots of handling during the **first week**.

When handling begins, scoop the skink gently with both hands and support all four legs. If the skink backs away or opens its mouth in a defensive display, try again another time.

## Routine cleaning

**Remove waste promptly and monitor the enclosure every day.** The RSPCA recommends spot-cleaning feces and urates as soon as they appear and carrying out a more complete enclosure clean approximately monthly using a reptile-safe disinfectant according to product directions.

Wash hands before and after reptile handling or enclosure cleaning.

## Health warning signs

General warning signs that deserve closer attention include:

- labored or open-mouth breathing;
- mucus or bubbles around the nostrils;
- marked weight loss;
- abnormal jaw shape or weakness;
- persistent retained shed with tissue damage;
- wounds, burns, or swelling;
- significant change in appetite or behavior.

These signs are not diagnoses. Review husbandry and seek reptile-veterinary advice when symptoms are persistent, severe, or worsening.

## Frequently asked questions
### What temperature does a blue-tongue skink need?

Use a basking zone of about 30–32°C, up to 35°C for younger skinks, with a cooler end around 22–26°C. Night temperature should remain around 20–22°C in the RSPCA care framework.

### What humidity does a blue-tongue skink need?

It depends on species and locality. The RSPCA gives a broad 40–90% range across blue-tongue skinks rather than one universal number.

### Does a blue-tongue skink need UVB?

The RSPCA recommends a UVI gradient of 3.0–5.0 in the basking zone to zero in shade.

### What should an adult blue-tongue skink eat?

Approximately 50–60% animal matter and 40–50% vegetables, with fruit no more than 10% of the diet, according to the RSPCA care sheet.
