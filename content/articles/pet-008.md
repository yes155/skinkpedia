# Peter's Banded Skink Guide: Care, Substrate, Diet & Buying Notes

**Peter's Banded Skink (*Scincopus fasciatus*) is a nocturnal, fossorial desert skink from North Africa.** For broader species-selection and ownership context, see the [Pet Skinks guide](/pet-skinks/). It spends much of the day below loose substrate and becomes more active after dark. Care should prioritize deep diggable substrate, a warm hide, cooler retreat, low average humidity with a humid microclimate, appropriate UVB, and a varied omnivorous/invertebrate-heavy feeding plan.

## Size and natural history

Veterinary and specialist care references place adults roughly in the **5–10 inch** total-length range, with published care sheets differing because wild-caught stock and locality data are inconsistent.

PetMD gives **8–10 inches** for adult Peter's Banded Skinks. The Bio Dude lists smaller 5–7 inch adults in its current husbandry guide. For enclosure planning, use the animal's actual adult body size and provide generous floor area rather than selecting a minimal cage from a single length estimate.

## Enclosure

A practical adult baseline is **36 × 18 × 18 inches (90 × 45 × 45 cm)** or larger.

The enclosure should provide:

- deep loose substrate;
- a warm hide;
- a cooler hide;
- secure surface cover;
- low-level UVB;
- clean drinking water;
- measured temperature and humidity;
- escape prevention.

## Substrate

**Use at least about 4–6 inches (10–15 cm) of loose sand or sandy soil**, with deeper substrate where the enclosure allows.

Reptile Supply uses a 4-inch minimum, while the Bio Dude recommends at least 6 inches for a bioactive setup. Both agree that the substrate should support normal digging behavior.

Provide a localized humid hide rather than keeping the whole enclosure humid.

## Temperature

Reptile Supply recommends:

- **warm hide:** about **90–95°F (32–35°C)**;
- **cool side:** about **75–80°F (24–27°C)**;
- **night:** can fall to around **65°F (18°C)** in a stable setup.

Measure the warm hide directly rather than assuming the heat-lamp wattage produces the correct temperature.

## Humidity

Keep average enclosure humidity **below about 50%**, with a humid hide around **70% or higher** as a localized shedding/hydration refuge.

## UVB and day/night cycle

Although this species is nocturnal, current specialist guides recommend access to low-level high-quality UVB and a clear day/night lighting cycle.

Because no strong species-specific UVI study establishes one exact target, use a low-output linear UVB setup at a safe measured distance with shade and hiding opportunities rather than inventing a numerical UVI requirement.

## Diet

Peter's Banded Skinks are treated as omnivorous in current husbandry references, with invertebrates forming an important part of captive feeding.

Offer a varied diet and feed near the active evening period when practical.

Avoid rigid percentages unless supported by a direct species nutrition source.

## Buying and origin

**Ask for the exact origin of the animal rather than guessing from price.**

Current specialist sources note that wild-caught animals remain common in the trade, which makes quarantine, veterinary examination, and parasite screening especially relevant when origin is uncertain.

Ask the seller:

- captive-bred, captive-hatched, farmed, imported, or wild-caught?
- approximate age and time in captivity?
- current feeding history?
- recent fecal testing or veterinary care?
- current body weight and photographs?
- any known injuries or treatment?

Do not automatically medicate or deworm a new skink without veterinary guidance.

## Frequently asked questions

### How big do Peter's Banded Skinks get?

Published husbandry sources vary; PetMD gives about 8–10 inches for adults, while some specialist care sheets report smaller animals. Use the actual animal's size for enclosure planning.

### How deep should the substrate be?

At least about 4–6 inches of loose sand or sandy soil, with deeper substrate preferred where practical.

### What temperature do they need?

A warm hide around 90–95°F (32–35°C) and a cool side around 75–80°F (24–27°C).

### What humidity do they need?

Low average humidity below about 50%, plus a localized humid hide.

### Do Peter's Banded Skinks need UVB?

Current care guides recommend providing low-level high-quality UVB despite the species' nocturnal activity.

### Are Peter's Banded Skinks wild-caught?

Wild-caught animals remain common in current specialist trade guidance, but verify the origin of the individual animal rather than assuming.
