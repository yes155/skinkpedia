# What Do Skinks Eat? Diet, Feeding & Nutrition by Species

**Skinks do not all eat the same diet.** Depending on the species, a skink may be primarily insectivorous, omnivorous, or strongly herbivorous. The safest way to feed a pet skink is to identify the exact species first, then match its food categories, feeding frequency, and supplementation to evidence for that species rather than following one universal “skink diet.”

For the broader husbandry framework, see the [skink care guide](/skink-care/). This guide explains the general feeding framework—diet type, food categories, frequency principles, supplementation, and hydration—while species pages provide exact ratios and schedules where evidence supports them.

## What do skinks eat?

**Many skinks eat insects and other invertebrates, but the family Scincidae includes omnivorous and herbivorous species as well.**

A useful first step is to classify the species into its main feeding pattern:

| Diet type | What it means | Examples in this site |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Insectivorous** | Diet is based mainly on insects and other invertebrate prey | Fire skinks, red-eyed crocodile skinks |
| **Omnivorous** | Diet combines animal matter with plant foods | Blue-tongue skinks |
| **Herbivorous** | Diet is dominated by leaves and other plant material | Prehensile-tailed skinks |

These are broad categories, not interchangeable feeding plans. Exact foods, proportions, and feeding schedules still depend on the species.

## Feeding insectivorous skinks

**Insectivorous skinks should receive an appropriate variety of captive feeder invertebrates rather than relying on one feeder species.**

Common feeder categories used in captive reptile diets include:

- crickets;
- roaches;
- mealworms or similar larvae;
- other appropriately sized commercially raised invertebrates.

Choose prey that is:

- small enough for the skink to swallow safely;
- sourced from a reliable captive feeder supplier;
- nutritionally prepared before feeding;
- removed if uneaten and likely to disturb the animal.

### Gut-loading feeder insects

**Gut-loading means feeding feeder insects a nutritious diet before they are offered to the skink.** The purpose is to improve the nutritional quality of the prey rather than treating an insect as a nutritionally complete food by itself.

Use a feeder diet appropriate to the insect species and avoid wild-caught insects from areas where pesticide exposure, parasites, or species identification are uncertain.

For a species-specific example, see the [red-eyed crocodile skink care guide](/pet-skinks/red-eyed-crocodile-skink/care/).

## Feeding omnivorous skinks

**Omnivorous skinks need both animal and plant foods, but the correct balance depends on the species.** Do not apply one percentage ratio to every omnivorous skink.

Blue-tongue skinks provide a well-documented example. The RSPCA blue-tongue care guidance describes an adult diet of approximately:

- **50–60% animal matter**;
- **40–50% vegetables**;
- **fruit at no more than 10% of the diet**.

Those figures apply to blue-tongue skinks and should not be converted into a universal Scincidae formula.

For the complete blue-tongue food list, ratios, and feeding chart, use the [blue-tongue skink diet guide](/pet-skinks/blue-tongue-skink/diet/).

## Feeding herbivorous skinks

**Some skinks are strongly plant-eating and should not be fed as though they were blue-tongue skinks or insectivores.**

The prehensile-tailed skink (*Corucia zebrata*) is an important example of a herbivorous skink. Its diet is based on plant material rather than routine insect feeding.

For herbivorous species, the key decisions are:

- which leafy or browse-type foods are appropriate;
- how much dietary variety is needed;
- how calcium and other nutrients are supplied;
- which foods are unsuitable or should be limited;
- how feeding changes with age, reproduction, and body condition.

Do not add animal protein simply because another skink species eats it.

## How often should you feed a skink?

**Feeding frequency depends on species, age, growth rate, food type, body condition, and reproductive state.** There is no safe universal schedule such as “all juvenile skinks eat daily” or “all adults eat three times a week.”

Before setting a schedule, verify:

1. the exact species;
2. juvenile vs. adult status;
3. whether the diet is insectivorous, omnivorous, or herbivorous;
4. the energy density of the foods being offered;
5. current body condition and weight trend;
6. whether the animal is breeding, recovering, or otherwise physiologically different from a typical adult.

Species pages should supply the actual feeding schedule. This general page should not override them.

## Calcium and vitamin supplementation

**Supplementation is useful only when it is matched to the species, diet, UV exposure, life stage, and the nutritional content of the foods being offered.**

Calcium powders and reptile multivitamins are common husbandry tools, but one dusting frequency should not be applied across Scincidae.

Before using a supplement, confirm:

- whether the species requires routine supplementation;
- whether the animal receives UVB and, if so, at what verified level;
- whether the supplement contains vitamin D3;
- how often the species-specific source recommends its use;
- whether other foods already contain added vitamins or minerals;
- whether the animal is juvenile, adult, gravid, or under veterinary care.

More is not automatically better. Vitamins and minerals can be over-supplemented, so follow species-specific evidence and product directions rather than estimating doses.

For equipment and supplement categories, see the [skink supplies checklist](/skink-care/supplies-checklist/).

## Calcium-to-phosphorus balance

**Calcium is essential to normal skeletal function, but supplementation should be considered as part of the whole diet rather than as a substitute for poor food selection.**

A sound feeding plan combines:

- appropriate staple foods;
- suitable feeder preparation where insects are used;
- species-specific supplementation;
- appropriate UV exposure where required;
- monitoring of growth and body condition.

If a skink develops weakness, abnormal jaw shape, tremors, difficulty moving, or other possible signs of nutritional disease, the correct next step is veterinary assessment rather than simply increasing supplement doses.

See the [skink health guide](/skink-care/health/) for warning signs and escalation.

## Safe food selection: use categories, not universal lists

**A food that is suitable for one skink species is not automatically suitable for another.**

Evaluate foods by asking:

- Does this species naturally eat animal matter, plant matter, or both?
- Is the food appropriate as a staple, occasional item, or treat?
- Is the item nutritionally balanced enough for repeated use?
- Is it safely sized and prepared?
- Is it free from pesticide or contaminant concerns?
- Does a reliable species-specific source actually recommend it?

This is especially important with foods often copied between reptile care sheets, including fruit, eggs, prepared pet foods, greens, and high-fat feeder insects.

## Foods to avoid: evidence before blanket bans

**Do not create a universal “toxic skink food” list by copying claims from unrelated reptile species.** Some foods are genuinely unsuitable, but the reason and level of risk should be supported for the species or at least for reptiles generally by a reliable source.

A practical rule is to avoid:

- unidentified wild-caught insects;
- food exposed to pesticides or other chemicals;
- spoiled or moldy food;
- prey that is too large to be swallowed safely;
- heavily processed foods not supported by the species’ care guidance;
- any item specifically contraindicated by a reliable species-specific source.

Where a food is merely high in sugar, fat, oxalates, or another nutrient, that does not automatically mean it is acutely toxic. Use precise wording such as **limit**, **occasional**, **avoid**, or **toxic** only when the evidence supports that category.

## Do skinks drink water?

**Yes, captive skinks should have access to clean drinking water unless a species-specific husbandry source gives a different method of hydration.**

Use a stable dish that the animal can access safely. Keep the water clean, remove contamination promptly, and place the dish where it does not undermine the enclosure’s required substrate or humidity conditions.

Hydration is not only about drinking. Some species also depend on humid microclimates or moisture-rich foods, so water management should be coordinated with the [skink enclosure setup guide](/skink-care/enclosure-setup/).

## Can skinks eat fruit?

**Some omnivorous and herbivorous skinks can eat fruit, but fruit should not be treated as a universal staple.**

For blue-tongue skinks, the RSPCA adult diet guidance limits fruit to **no more than 10%** of the diet. That is a blue-tongue value, not a general rule for every skink.

If your species-specific care source does not identify fruit as an appropriate food category, do not add it simply because another skink eats fruit.

## Do skinks like bananas?

**Whether banana is appropriate depends on the species and the rest of the diet.** A skink accepting a food does not prove that the food is nutritionally appropriate as a staple.

For species such as blue-tongue skinks, fruit already belongs to a limited portion of the diet. Use the species-specific food guide rather than treating banana as a standard skink food.

## What is a skink’s favorite food?

**There is no single favorite food for skinks because Scincidae contains species with very different feeding ecologies.** An insectivorous skink and a herbivorous skink should not be expected to prefer or require the same foods.

A better question is: **what foods should this exact species receive regularly?** Use that species’ care sheet as the primary source.

## Building a feeding routine

A practical feeding routine should answer five questions:

1. **What species is this?**
2. **What diet category does the species belong to?**
3. **Which foods are appropriate staples?**
4. **How often should this life stage be fed?**
5. **What supplementation, if any, is supported by the species’ care source?**

Then track:

- appetite;
- body condition;
- weight trend where practical;
- stool quality;
- growth in juveniles;
- leftover food;
- feeding behavior.

If the animal is gaining or losing condition unexpectedly, do not automatically change the diet by guesswork. Review the husbandry and seek reptile-veterinary advice when needed.

## Feeding equipment by function

Product choices should follow the nutritional requirement, not lead it.

| Need | Useful equipment category |
| --- | --- |
| Offering live prey | Appropriately sized feeder container or feeding tongs where suitable |
| Preparing plant foods | Dedicated chopping surface and washable food dish |
| Supplementation | Species-appropriate calcium or multivitamin product |
| Water access | Stable, easy-to-clean water dish |
| Weight monitoring | Digital gram scale where practical |
| Feeder preparation | Suitable feeder housing and gut-loading food |

Choose products only after you know what the species actually needs.

## Frequently asked questions
### What should I feed a pet skink?

Identify the species first. Insectivorous skinks need appropriate invertebrate prey, omnivorous skinks need both animal and plant foods, and herbivorous skinks need a plant-based diet. Exact foods and schedules belong to species-specific guidance.

### Can all skinks eat the same insects?

No. Prey size, feeder type, and whether insects belong in the diet at all depend on the species.

### How often should I dust skink food with calcium?

There is no universal schedule for all skinks. Supplement frequency should follow the species, age, diet, UV exposure, and the product being used.

### Can pet skinks eat dog or cat food?

Do not treat dog or cat food as a universal skink staple. Whether a prepared animal-protein food is appropriate depends on the species and the evidence supporting its use. Follow the species-specific diet guide.

### Do skinks need fresh water every day?

Captive skinks should have clean water available and the dish should be refreshed whenever it becomes dirty. Exact hydration strategy can vary by species and enclosure design.
