# Ocellated Skink Guide: Care, Enclosure, Diet, Size & Health

**The Ocellated Skink (*Chalcides ocellatus*) is a small, diurnal, semi-fossorial skink found across Mediterranean, North African, and Middle Eastern habitats.** For a broader pet-species comparison, see the [Pet Skinks guide](/pet-skinks/). Good captive care uses loose sandy substrate, a strong basking surface, cooler retreat, a pronounced day/night humidity cycle, measured UVB, varied invertebrate prey, and cautious monitoring if animals are housed together.

## Size and appearance

Adults are typically about **7–9 inches (17–23 cm)** total length.

The species has a long cylindrical body, short limbs, smooth scales, and dark eye-like spots or mottling over bronze, tan, or brown coloration.

## Enclosure size

For one adult, current specialist guidance uses **about 30 × 12 × 12 inches (75 × 30 × 30 cm) as a minimum**, with **36 × 18 × 18 inches (90 × 45 × 45 cm) or larger preferred**.

More usable floor area improves thermal choice, enrichment, and group-management options.

## Substrate

**Provide about 3–4 inches (7–10 cm) of fine sand or sandy soil** so the skink can dig and partially bury itself.

Keep heavy rocks and furnishings stable so they cannot shift if substrate is excavated.

## Heating

Current ReptiFiles guidance recommends:

- **basking surface:** about **110–120°F (43–49°C)**;
- **basking air:** about **90–100°F (33–37°C)**;
- **cool side:** about **75–85°F (24–29°C)**;
- **night:** about **65–70°F (18–21°C)**.

The distinction between surface and air temperature matters. Measure the basking surface with an infrared thermometer and air temperature with digital probes.

## Humidity cycle

**Ocellated Skinks benefit from a dry daytime environment followed by a strong nighttime humidity rise.**

ReptiFiles recommends approximately:

- **day:** **15–35%**;
- **night:** **90–100%** after evening misting.

Good ventilation is essential so the enclosure dries again during the day.

## UVB

ReptiFiles places the species in **Ferguson Zone 2** and recommends a measured **UVI around 2.0–3.0 at the basking surface**, with lower exposure and shade elsewhere.

## Diet

**Ocellated Skinks are primarily insectivorous.**

Offer a varied rotation of appropriately sized feeder invertebrates. Gut-load prey and follow supplement labels rather than applying one fixed dusting schedule to every animal.

Some natural-history sources record occasional plant material, but insects should remain the main captive-food category unless a species-specific feeding plan supports otherwise.

## Cohabitation

Ocellated Skinks can sometimes be housed in compatible groups, but communal housing is optional and introduces conflict and breeding risks.

If housing more than one:

- use additional floor space;
- avoid male-male combinations;
- provide multiple hides and basking options;
- monitor feeding access and body condition;
- keep a separate enclosure available.

Single housing is the safer default when sex or compatibility is uncertain.

## Health monitoring

Watch for:

- unexplained weight loss;
- persistent appetite loss;
- abnormal breathing;
- wounds or bite injuries;
- severe retained shed;
- weakness or abnormal movement;
- repeated exclusion from heat or food in group housing.

For clinical warning signs and veterinary escalation, see the [skink health guide](/skink-care/health/).

## Frequently asked questions

### How big do Ocellated Skinks get?

About 7–9 inches (17–23 cm) total length.

### What substrate do they need?

About 3–4 inches of fine sand or sandy soil that allows digging and partial burial.

### What temperature do Ocellated Skinks need?

A basking surface around 110–120°F (43–49°C), with cooler air around 75–85°F (24–29°C).

### What humidity do they need?

A dry daytime range around 15–35%, followed by a strong nighttime rise after misting.

### What UVI do Ocellated Skinks need?

ReptiFiles recommends around UVI 2.0–3.0 at the basking surface, with shade available.

### Can Ocellated Skinks live together?

Sometimes, but group housing requires compatible animals, extra resources, monitoring, and the ability to separate them.
