# Cullen Skink: Traditional Scottish Smoked Haddock Soup + Recipe

**Cullen skink is a thick Scottish soup made with smoked haddock, potatoes, onion, and milk.** It comes from **Cullen in Moray, northeast Scotland**, where smoked haddock became the defining ingredient. Recipes vary in richness and technique, but the central entity remains the same: **smoked haddock → potatoes → onion → milk → thick, smoky soup**.

> **Disambiguation:** Cullen skink is a food and has nothing to do with skink lizards. For the word’s reptile, verb, and Scottish soup meanings, see [Skink Meaning](/meaning/skink-meaning/).

## What is Cullen skink?

**Cullen skink is a traditional Scottish smoked-haddock soup associated with the coastal town of Cullen.**

The core ingredients are:

- smoked haddock;
- potatoes;
- onion;
- milk;
- butter in many versions;
- parsley or chives as an optional finish.

Some cooks add cream, but cream is not required for the soup to be rich or thick.

## Why is it called Cullen skink?

The name has two parts:

- **Cullen** — the Moray coastal town associated with the dish;
- **skink** — an older Scots word used for soup or broth.

Scots-language records show *skink* being used for soup well before the modern smoked-haddock dish became famous.

Historical accounts of Cullen skink describe an earlier beef-broth tradition and the later use of locally available smoked haddock as the characteristic Cullen version.

## What fish is used in Cullen skink?

**Smoked haddock is the defining fish.**

Traditional recipes often specify **undyed smoked haddock**. “Finnan haddie” is a well-known Scottish smoked-haddock style and can be used when available.

Fresh unsmoked haddock will produce a different soup because the smoked flavor is central to Cullen skink.

## Cullen skink ingredients

### Serves 4

- **350 g smoked haddock**, preferably undyed
- **500 g floury potatoes**, peeled and cut into small cubes
- **1 medium onion**, finely chopped
- **500 ml whole milk**
- **250 ml water**
- **25 g butter**
- **black pepper**, to taste
- **2 tbsp chopped parsley or chives**, optional

Smoked haddock can already be salty, so taste before adding salt.

## How to make Cullen skink

### 1. Soften the onion

Melt the butter in a saucepan over medium-low heat. Add the onion and cook for about **5–7 minutes**, until soft but not browned.

### 2. Cook the potatoes

Add the potatoes and water. Bring to a gentle simmer and cook for about **10–15 minutes**, or until the potatoes are tender.

### 3. Poach the smoked haddock

While the potatoes cook, place the smoked haddock in another pan with the milk.

Bring the milk to a very gentle simmer and cook the fish for roughly **4–5 minutes**, until it flakes easily.

Lift out the haddock and reserve the milk.

### 4. Flake the fish

When cool enough to handle, remove any skin and bones and break the haddock into large flakes.

### 5. Thicken the soup with potato

Pour the reserved poaching milk into the potato-and-onion pan.

Mash some of the potatoes against the side of the pan while leaving the rest in pieces. This gives Cullen skink its thick texture without requiring flour.

### 6. Add the haddock

Fold in most of the smoked haddock and warm gently for another **2–3 minutes**.

Do not boil hard after adding the milk and fish.

### 7. Serve

Taste and season with black pepper. Ladle into bowls and finish with:

- remaining haddock flakes;
- chopped parsley or chives;
- crusty bread or oatcakes on the side.

## Should Cullen skink contain cream?

**It can, but it does not have to.**

Milk, potatoes, and smoked haddock are enough to create a creamy texture. A small amount of double cream can be added at the end for a richer version.

The more useful distinction is not “authentic vs fake,” but whether **smoked haddock remains the dominant flavor**.

## How thick should Cullen skink be?

Cullen skink should be **thicker than a light fish broth but still spoonable as a soup**.

If it is too thin:

- mash more potato into the liquid;
- simmer gently for a few minutes.

If it is too thick:

- add a little milk.

This uses the potato itself as the primary thickener.

## Cullen skink vs. fish chowder

| Attribute | Cullen skink | Typical fish chowder |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Defining fish** | Smoked haddock | Varies: cod, haddock, mixed seafood, etc. |
| **Core starch** | Potatoes | Usually potatoes |
| **Main liquid** | Milk, often with fish-poaching liquid | Milk/cream and/or stock |
| **Typical thickening** | Potato contributes much of the body | May use flour, cream, potato, or combinations |
| **Signature flavor** | Smoked haddock | Depends on fish and recipe |
| **Scottish origin** | Yes — Cullen, Moray | No single Scottish origin |

Cullen skink is often compared with chowder because both are creamy fish-and-potato soups, but the smoked haddock and Cullen origin make it a distinct dish.

## Can you make Cullen skink ahead of time?

**Yes.** Cool the soup promptly, refrigerate it, and reheat gently until steaming hot.

For best texture:

- avoid repeated reheating;
- do not boil aggressively;
- add a splash of milk if it thickens in the refrigerator.

Follow normal food-safety guidance for cooked fish and dairy.

## What to serve with Cullen skink

Common accompaniments include:

- crusty bread;
- oatcakes;
- buttered bread;
- a simple green salad if serving it as a main meal.

Because the soup is already rich and smoky, the side dish does not need to be elaborate.

Some recipes do, but milk alone is common and sufficient. Cream is an optional enrichment.

Cullen skink is specifically associated with smoked haddock and Cullen, Scotland. Chowder is a broader category with many fish, seafood, thickening, and regional variations.

Crusty bread and oatcakes are both good accompaniments.
