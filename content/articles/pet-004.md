# Sandfish Skink Guide: Desert Care, Sand Depth, Heating & Diet

**The Sandfish Skink (*Scincus scincus*) is a small, diurnal desert skink specialized for moving through loose sand.** For suitability and species-selection context, see the [Pet Skinks guide](/pet-skinks/). Current species-specific husbandry guidance emphasizes deep clean sand, a very hot basking **surface**, a cooler retreat, low background humidity, low-to-moderate measured UVB, and an insect-based diet.

This species is unrelated to the protected Florida Sand Skink (*Plestiodon reynoldsi*).

## Size and lifespan

Current ReptiFiles guidance gives:

- **adult total length:** about **6–8 inches (15–20 cm)**;
- **average captive lifespan:** about **6–10 years**;
- **activity:** diurnal;
- **diet:** insectivorous.

## Sand-swimming adaptations

Sandfish move below the surface using body undulation. Their wedge-shaped snout, smooth scales, compact limbs, and body profile reduce resistance as they move through loose substrate.

## Enclosure and sand depth

**Provide at least about 4–6 inches (10–15 cm) of clean, fine, diggable sand.**

The enclosure must be escape-resistant and large enough to create a usable hot-to-cool gradient. Surface structures such as rocks or hides should be stable and arranged so they cannot collapse if the skink excavates beneath nearby sand.

A shallow water dish can be offered.

## Heating: surface temperature vs. air temperature

**Sandfish husbandry requires unusually hot basking surfaces, but that number must not be confused with enclosure air temperature.**

Current ReptiFiles guidance gives:

- **basking surface:** about **130–140°F (54–60°C)**;
- **cool-side air:** about **80–90°F (26–32°C)**;
- **night:** about **70–80°F (21–26°C)**.

Measure the basking **surface** with an infrared thermometer and track air temperature separately with digital probes.

The skink must always be able to retreat into cooler substrate and shaded areas.

## UVB

ReptiFiles places Sandfish Skinks in **Ferguson Zone 1** and recommends a measured **UVI around 1.0–2.0 at the basking surface**, decreasing elsewhere so the animal can self-regulate exposure.

Lamp percentage alone is not the target; output depends on fixture, distance, mesh, and enclosure design.

## Humidity

Sandfish live in arid habitat. Keep the enclosure predominantly dry rather than maintaining a tropical humidity level.

A localized moisture gradient deeper in the substrate can occur naturally, but the surface should not remain continuously damp.

## Diet

**Sandfish Skinks are insectivorous.** Offer varied, appropriately sized captive invertebrates.

ReptiFiles uses age-dependent feeding frequency, with adults generally fed about every other day. Adjust intake to body condition and prey size.

Gut-load feeders and use supplements according to product directions and UVB provision.

## Handling

Sandfish spend much of their time below the substrate. They are best treated as observation-oriented animals rather than reptiles that need frequent handling.

Avoid repeatedly digging them out for interaction. When handling is necessary, support the body and never pull the tail.

## Frequently asked questions

### How big do Sandfish Skinks get?

About 6–8 inches (15–20 cm) total length.

### How deep should the sand be?

At least about 4–6 inches (10–15 cm) of clean, fine, diggable sand.

### What basking temperature do Sandfish Skinks need?

Current guidance uses a **surface** temperature around 130–140°F (54–60°C), with cooler air on the opposite side. Do not treat that surface value as the enclosure's ambient air temperature.

### What UVI do Sandfish Skinks need?

ReptiFiles recommends roughly UVI 1.0–2.0 at the basking surface, with lower exposure and shade elsewhere.

### What do Sandfish Skinks eat?

Primarily captive feeder insects and other appropriate invertebrates.

### How long do Sandfish Skinks live?

Current ReptiFiles guidance gives an average captive lifespan of about 6–10 years.
