# Western Skink Guide: Identification, Range, Habitat, Diet & Life Cycle

**The Western Skink (*Plestiodon skiltonianus*) is a small western North American skink whose juveniles are especially recognizable by their vivid blue tails and longitudinal body stripes.** It occurs from southern British Columbia through parts of the western United States and uses habitats that combine open sun with nearby soil, rock, wood, or vegetation cover.

This guide follows **range → habitat → ecology → life cycle**. State-level detail belongs in pages such as [Skinks in California](/wild-skinks/california/) and the broader [North American skink directory](/wild-skinks/north-america/).

## Western Skink range

**Western Skinks occur across a broad part of western North America.**

Available research includes:

- southern British Columbia;
- Washington;
- Oregon;
- California;
- Nevada;
- Utah;
- Idaho and adjoining western regions.

Exact local distribution depends on subspecies and habitat, so a state-specific range map should not be inferred from this general page.

## How do you identify a Western Skink?

**Juveniles typically have a dark body with pale longitudinal stripes and a bright blue tail.**

Identification references describe four prominent light stripes. As animals mature:

- the tail becomes less vividly blue;
- body coloration becomes more muted;
- stripes may become less distinct.

Because juvenile Western Skinks resemble other blue-tailed *Plestiodon*, geography is one of the strongest first identification clues.

## Western Skink size

**Published evidence places adult total length at roughly 6–8 inches (15–21 cm).**

This is smaller than the largest Broad-headed Skinks but large enough that adults may appear substantially more robust than hatchlings.

## Where do Western Skinks live?

**Western Skinks use a mosaic of grassland, chaparral, woodland edge, and rocky habitat, usually with accessible shelter and suitable soil moisture.**

Common cover features include:

- flat rocks;
- logs;
- leaf litter;
- loose soil;
- woody debris;
- vegetation along habitat edges.

A better description is that Western Skinks use **microhabitats with shelter and moisture within otherwise variable western landscapes**, including relatively dry regions.

## What do Western Skinks eat?

**Western Skinks feed mainly on small invertebrates.**

Published references list prey such as:

- spiders;
- beetles;
- moths;
- grasshoppers;
- crickets;
- sowbugs;
- earthworms.

Avoid describing their jaws as “delicate” or claiming they intentionally avoid all hard prey unless a dietary study directly supports that distinction.

## Reproduction and life cycle

**Western Skinks are egg-laying, and females remain associated with the nest.**

Published evidence gives a clutch of approximately **2–10 eggs**, laid in protected sites such as soil cavities or beneath cover.

Juveniles emerge with the characteristic bright blue tail, which becomes less vivid as they mature.

## Tail loss

Western Skinks can use tail autotomy as a defense.

A lost tail can regenerate, but regrowth has energetic and structural costs. It should not be described as automatically causing death or as an event with no consequence.

For the full mechanism, see [skink tail autotomy and regrowth](/skink-biology/tail-drop-regrowth/).

## Can you keep a Western Skink as a pet?

**Do not collect a wild Western Skink without first checking the law and the species’ status in your location.** Wildlife rules vary by state and province.

Wild animals also do not come with the predictable feeding and husbandry history of captive-bred pet skinks.

Readers looking for a pet should start with the [pet skink species guide](/pet-skinks/).

## Frequently asked questions
### Where are Western Skinks found?

They occur from southern British Columbia through much of the western United States, including Washington, Oregon, California, Nevada, Utah, and nearby regions.

### How big do Western Skinks get?

The published evidence gives approximately 6–8 inches total length.

### Why do young Western Skinks have blue tails?

The conspicuous tail appears to help redirect predator attacks toward a detachable part of the body.

### What do Western Skinks eat?

They eat small invertebrates such as spiders, beetles, crickets, grasshoppers, sowbugs, and worms.

### Do Western Skinks guard their eggs?

Available research describes females remaining with clutches of roughly 2–10 eggs.

### Can I catch a Western Skink and keep it?

Wildlife collection rules vary by jurisdiction. Check local law first; for pet ownership, a legal captive-bred species is usually the more appropriate route.
