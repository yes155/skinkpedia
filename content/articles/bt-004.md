# Blue-Tongue Skink Diet: Food List, Feeding Chart & Nutrition

**Adult blue-tongue skinks are omnivores and should receive a varied diet containing approximately 50–60% animal matter and 40–50% vegetables, with fruit making up no more than 10%.** Those proportions come from the RSPCA blue-tongue skink care sheet and are more reliable than the commonly repeated 50% vegetables / 40% protein / 10% fruit formula.

This guide covers **food categories, feeding structure, gut-loading, supplementation, hydration, and body-condition monitoring**. For the full daily husbandry framework around feeding, temperature, handling and health, see the [Blue-Tongue Skink Care Guide](/pet-skinks/blue-tongue-skink/care/).

## Blue-tongue skink diet ratio

For adults, the RSPCA recommends:

| Diet component | Approximate share |
| --- | ---: |
| **Animal matter** | **50–60%** |
| **Vegetables** | **40–50%** |
| **Fruit** | **No more than 10%** |

This is an adult feeding framework. Young skinks grow quickly and the RSPCA recommends a “little and often” approach rather than a rigid universal tablespoon schedule.

## Animal foods

**Offer a varied range of safe animal foods rather than relying on one staple.**

The RSPCA lists options such as:

- appropriately sized crickets;
- calciworms;
- waxmoth larvae;
- fruit beetle grubs;
- dubia cockroaches;
- silkworms;
- locusts;
- live or canned snails from safe reptile-food sources;
- eggs;
- selected meat such as ground turkey, chicken, or lean beef;
- an appropriate canned dog food where suitable;
- frozen-thawed pinky rats or fuzzy mice only as treats.

Uneaten crickets or locusts should be removed because they can bite the skink.

## Snails

**Snails are an important food item in blue-tongue feeding ecology and can add both dietary variety and foraging enrichment.** The RSPCA recommends live or canned snails obtained from reptile shops or other controlled sources.

Do not collect snails from outdoors because pesticide or parasite exposure may be unknown.

## Vegetables and greens

**Vegetables should form a substantial part of the adult diet.** Use a varied rotation of safe plant foods rather than relying heavily on one green.

The RSPCA specifically advises:

- researching safe vegetables and plants;
- avoiding **too much spinach**, because it can interfere with calcium absorption;
- avoiding **too much kale**, because excessive amounts may affect hormone function.

This is different from declaring spinach or kale universally “toxic.” The evidence supports moderation, not an acute-poison label.

## Fruit

**Fruit should form no more than 10% of the adult diet.** Treat it as a limited dietary component rather than the base of the meal.

Use only fruits confirmed as safe for the species, and remove uneaten fresh food before it spoils.

## Gut-loading feeder insects

**Gut-load feeder insects before offering them to the skink.** The RSPCA recommends feeding insects safe vegetables and an appropriate formulated gut-loading diet approximately **24–48 hours before feeding**.

This improves the nutritional quality of the insects and should be combined with appropriate supplementation.

## Calcium and vitamins

**Supplementation should be light, deliberate, and matched to the animal’s diet and UVB setup.** The RSPCA advises dusting food with supplement powders and consulting a reptile veterinarian when needed.

Important points:

- vitamins and minerals can be overdosed;
- always follow product instructions;
- a well-designed UVB system reduces the need for high dietary vitamin D3 levels;
- do not invent a universal “calcium every meal” or “multivitamin every two weeks” rule unless a species-specific veterinary source supports it.

## Feeding chart by life stage

The published evidence supports a principle-based chart rather than unsupported fixed portion sizes:

| Life stage | Feeding approach | What to monitor |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Young / rapidly growing** | “Little and often” according to the RSPCA; provide a varied, balanced diet | Growth, weight trend, appetite, body condition |
| **Subadult** | Gradually transition toward the adult feeding pattern as growth slows | Weight and body condition |
| **Adult** | Use the 50–60% animal / 40–50% vegetable framework with fruit ≤10%; adjust feeding frequency to body condition | Obesity risk, monthly weight, activity |

## Obesity and weight monitoring

**Blue-tongue skinks can become obese in captivity.** The RSPCA links obesity risk to high-protein diets and restricted exercise and recommends weighing the skink approximately **once a month**.

Track:

- body weight;
- body condition;
- appetite;
- activity;
- feeding composition.

Do not react to one weight measurement alone; look at the trend over time.

## Water and hydration

**Provide clean, fresh water at all times in a large, shallow dish.** Replace the water daily and whenever it is contaminated.

Some animals may also drink water droplets after light enclosure spraying, depending on the humidity plan for the exact taxon.

## Formulated omnivore diets

**An appropriate formulated reptile diet for omnivores can be used as an alternative feeding option where the product provides the correct plant-to-animal balance.** Follow the manufacturer’s directions and make sure the product is intended for omnivorous reptiles rather than treating any processed food as automatically suitable.

## Frequently asked questions
### What is the correct blue-tongue skink diet ratio?

For adults, approximately 50–60% animal matter and 40–50% vegetables, with fruit no more than 10%, according to the RSPCA.

### How often should I feed a baby blue-tongue skink?

The RSPCA describes young skinks as growing quickly and benefiting from a “little and often” approach. The published evidence does not support one universal tablespoon-based daily schedule.

### Should blue-tongue skinks eat snails?

The RSPCA identifies snails as an important food source and recommends live or canned snails from controlled reptile-food sources.

### Should I add vitamin D3 if I use UVB?

With a well-set UV system, the RSPCA notes that high dietary D3 levels are not needed. Follow species-specific veterinary and supplement guidance rather than guessing.

### Is spinach toxic to blue-tongue skinks?

The published evidence says to avoid feeding **too much spinach** because it interferes with calcium absorption. That supports moderation rather than describing spinach as an acutely toxic food.
