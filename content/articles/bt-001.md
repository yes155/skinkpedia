# Blue-Tongue Skink: Species, Size, Lifespan, Temperament & Facts

**Blue-tongue skinks are large, ground-dwelling skinks in the genus *Tiliqua*, a group within the family Scincidae.** Members of the genus occur across Australia, New Guinea, and parts of Indonesia and are best known for their broad heads, smooth scales, heavy bodies, and conspicuous blue tongues.

This page covers the **species entity and natural-history profile**: taxonomy, body size, lifespan, defensive behavior, range, reproduction, and temperament. For captive husbandry, use the [blue-tongue skink care guide](/pet-skinks/blue-tongue-skink/care/).

## What is a blue-tongue skink?

**A blue-tongue skink is a member of the genus *Tiliqua* within the skink family Scincidae.** Current taxonomy recognizes seven species in the genus, with additional subspecies and locality forms within some species.

Blue-tongues are generally robust skinks with:

- broad heads;
- smooth, overlapping scales;
- relatively short limbs;
- a long, heavy body;
- a conspicuous blue tongue used in defensive display.

Northern and Eastern blue-tongues are subspecies of *Tiliqua scincoides*, while Indonesian blue-tongues belong primarily to *Tiliqua gigas*. For a taxonomic comparison, see [blue-tongue skink types](/pet-skinks/blue-tongue-skink/types/).

## How big do blue-tongue skinks get?

**Blue-tongue skinks commonly reach about 50–60 cm (20–24 in) in total length, although adult size varies among species, subspecies, locality, sex, and individual.**

Scientific datasets often compare blue-tongues by snout-vent length (SVL), which excludes the tail. The comparative biomechanical dataset reports *Tiliqua scincoides* at roughly **262 mm average SVL**, illustrating the large body size of this group relative to many other skinks.

Avoid applying one exact adult weight to the entire genus unless the value is tied to a specific taxon and source.

## Why is the tongue blue?

**The blue tongue is an important visual component of the skink’s defensive display.** When threatened, a blue-tongue skink may flatten or raise the body, open the mouth, hiss, and expose the vivid tongue.

The display makes the head and mouth visually conspicuous and may help deter a predator long enough for the skink to avoid further conflict.

The tongue also participates in normal squamate chemosensory behavior, but the blue coloration is especially notable in defensive communication.

## Where do blue-tongue skinks live?

**Species in the genus *Tiliqua* occur across a wide range of habitats in Australia, New Guinea, and parts of Indonesia.** Habitat differs substantially across the genus, which is one reason captive humidity and enclosure management cannot be reduced to a single generic value.

Blue-tongues are primarily ground-oriented and use surface cover such as vegetation, logs, rocks, and other shelters. Exact habitat, climate, and locality differ among Northern, Eastern, Indonesian, Shingleback, Centralian, Western, Blotched, and other forms.

For identification and husbandry differences among these taxa, use the [blue-tongue skink types guide](/pet-skinks/blue-tongue-skink/types/).

## What do blue-tongue skinks eat in the wild?

**Blue-tongue skinks are omnivorous and consume both animal and plant matter.** The RSPCA blue-tongue care sheet notes a broad wild diet that can include plant material, insects, carrion, small vertebrates, and snails.

This broad feeding ecology is important in captivity, but exact food ratios and feeding routines belong on the [blue-tongue skink diet guide](/pet-skinks/blue-tongue-skink/diet/).

## Blue-tongue skink reproduction

**Blue-tongue skinks are live-bearing rather than egg-laying.** Females retain developing young internally and give birth to live offspring.

Litter size varies substantially among *Tiliqua* species and individual females, so a single universal litter-size figure should not be applied to the whole genus.

## How long do blue-tongue skinks live?

**Blue-tongue skinks can be long-lived reptiles in captivity.** The RSPCA care sheet gives a captive lifespan of approximately **10–20+ years**, making them a long-term ownership commitment.

Lifespan depends on species, genetics, husbandry, disease, injury, and access to appropriate veterinary care. The correct ownership question is therefore not simply “How long does a blue-tongue live?” but whether you can provide suitable care for potentially two decades.

## Blue-tongue skink temperament

**Blue-tongue skinks can become tolerant of routine human interaction, but temperament varies among species and individuals.** The RSPCA advises allowing a newly acquired skink time to settle and notes that blue-tongues can have a strong bite.

A skink that backs away, opens its mouth, hisses, or displays the tongue is communicating that it wants more distance. Calm handling should support the body and avoid grabbing or forcing interaction.

For practical handling guidance, see [skink handling, bites and temperament](/skink-care/handling-bites-temperament/).

## Are blue-tongue skinks good pets?

**A blue-tongue skink can be a suitable pet for a keeper who can provide the required adult enclosure, heating, UVB, diet, humidity, and long-term veterinary care.** They should not be treated as low-maintenance reptiles simply because many individuals tolerate handling.

## Frequently asked questions
### What is the scientific name of a blue-tongue skink?

Blue-tongue skinks belong to the genus *Tiliqua*. Individual species have their own binomial names, such as *Tiliqua scincoides* and *Tiliqua gigas*.

### How big is a full-grown blue-tongue skink?

Many blue-tongue skinks are around 50–60 cm (20–24 in) in total length, but exact adult size depends on the taxon.

### How long do blue-tongue skinks live?

The RSPCA gives approximately 10–20+ years in captivity.

### Are blue-tongue skinks docile?

Many individuals can become tolerant of routine interaction, but temperament is not identical across every species or animal.
