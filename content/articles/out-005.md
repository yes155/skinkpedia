# Little Brown (Ground) Skink Guide: Identification, Habitat, Diet & Reproduction

**The Little Brown Skink (*Scincella lateralis*), also called the Ground Skink, is a tiny North American skink that lives primarily within leaf litter and other ground-level cover.** Adults are only about 3–5 inches long, making the species easy to overlook even where it is common.

This guide follows **identification → ground-layer habitat → diet → nesting**. Broader regional identification belongs in [North American Skinks](/wild-skinks/north-america/).

## How big is a Little Brown Skink?

**Adults reach approximately 3–5 inches (7.5–12.5 cm) total length.**

That places *Scincella lateralis* among the smallest native North American lizards in the cited sources species set.

Its small size, smooth scales, and elongated body help it move rapidly through loose leaf litter.

## How do you identify a Ground Skink?

**The species is typically bronze-brown with a darker lateral stripe running along each side of the body.**

Useful identification traits include:

- small body size;
- smooth, glossy scales;
- brown to bronze dorsal coloration;
- dark stripe through the eye and along the side;
- no bright blue juvenile tail like the familiar five-lined skinks.

The exact shade can vary, so habitat and body form are useful supporting clues.

## Where do Little Brown Skinks live?

**Ground Skinks are strongly associated with the leaf-litter layer of forests, woodland edges, and similar sheltered ground habitat.**

They can be found:

- beneath fallen leaves;
- under logs;
- under bark or ground debris;
- in loose surface soil;
- in shaded gardens and woodland-edge habitat.

## How do Ground Skinks move?

**Rather than climbing high into trees, they usually escape by slipping through leaves and ground debris.**

Their movement can appear snake-like because the body is elongated and the legs are small.

This locomotion is well suited to moving through a dense ground layer where open running space is limited.

## What do Little Brown Skinks eat?

**Little Brown Skinks feed on very small invertebrates found within the ground layer.**

Published evidence includes:

- spiders;
- beetles;
- small crickets;
- termites;
- tiny worms;
- insect larvae and other small arthropods.

Because the animal is small, prey is correspondingly small.

## Little Brown Skink eggs and reproduction

**The species is oviparous and differs from many larger *Plestiodon* skinks because the female does not remain to guard the eggs.**

Available research gives:

- **clutch size: 1–7 eggs**
- **up to four clutches in a breeding season**
- eggs placed in protected moist micro-sites;
- **nest desertion after laying** rather than prolonged maternal attendance.

This makes *Scincella lateralis* a useful contrast with nest-attending five-lined and Broad-headed Skinks.

For broader comparisons of skink reproductive strategies, see [skink reproduction](/skink-biology/reproduction/).

## Are Little Brown Skinks poisonous?

**The published evidence does not classify *Scincella lateralis* as venomous.** Its very small body size also means a defensive bite is unlikely to resemble the bite of a large pet skink.

However, this page should not expand into dog/cat toxicity claims. Those questions belong on [Are Skinks Poisonous or Dangerous?](/skinks/are-skinks-poisonous-dangerous/).

## Ground Skinks in gardens and yards

Ground Skinks can occur close to homes where leaf litter, mulch, logs, stones, or other cover is available.

If you want to encourage native skinks:

- retain some ground cover;
- avoid unnecessary disturbance of logs and leaf litter;
- reduce broad pesticide use where practical;
- keep free-roaming pets from hunting wildlife.

For a full backyard habitat guide, see [Garden Skinks](/backyard-skinks/garden-skinks/).

## Can a Ground Skink be kept as a pet?

This page is primarily about a wild native species. Collection laws vary, and wild-caught animals should not be treated as default pets.

If you are interested in keeping skinks, use the [pet skink guide](/pet-skinks/) to compare captive-bred species with established husbandry.

## Frequently asked questions
### Is a Little Brown Skink the same as a Ground Skink?

Yes. *Scincella lateralis* is commonly called both the Little Brown Skink and the Ground Skink.

### How big do Ground Skinks get?

Adults are approximately 3–5 inches total length.

### What do Ground Skinks eat?

They eat tiny invertebrates such as spiders, beetles, crickets, termites, larvae, and small worms.

### Where do Little Brown Skinks live?

They live mainly in leaf litter and other sheltered ground-level habitat in forests, woodland edges, and similar environments.

### How many eggs does a Ground Skink lay?

Published evidence gives roughly 1–7 eggs per clutch, with females capable of producing several clutches in a season.

### Do female Ground Skinks guard their eggs?

No. Available reproductive evidence describes nest desertion after laying rather than prolonged maternal care.

### Are Little Brown Skinks poisonous?

They are not treated as venomous in the published evidence. Broader pet-safety questions belong on the [skink safety guide](/skinks/are-skinks-poisonous-dangerous/).
