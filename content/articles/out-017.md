# Skinks in Florida: Species, Identification, Range & Safety

**Florida has seven native skink species: the Coal Skink, Mole Skink, Common Five-lined Skink, Southeastern Five-lined Skink, Broad-headed Skink, Florida Sand Skink, and Little Brown/Ground Skink.** Florida also supports established non-native skinks, so body shape, locality, life stage, and pattern should be used together when identifying an animal.

For the broader regional context, see the [North American skink directory](/wild-skinks/north-america/).

## Which skink species are native to Florida?

**Seven native species are recognized in Florida.**

| Species | Scientific name | Main field clue | Strongest Florida context |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Coal Skink** | *Plestiodon anthracinus* | Dark lateral band bordered by pale lines | Localized woodland and stream-associated habitats |
| **Mole Skink** | *Plestiodon egregius* | Small, fossorial skink; appearance varies among subspecies | Sandy habitats across several Florida regions |
| **Common Five-lined Skink** | *Plestiodon fasciatus* | Five pale stripes; blue tail in juveniles | Northern and parts of peninsular Florida |
| **Southeastern Five-lined Skink** | *Plestiodon inexpectatus* | Similar to Common Five-lined; more uniform subcaudal scales | Widespread southeastern habitats |
| **Broad-headed Skink** | *Plestiodon laticeps* | Large; breeding males develop broad orange-red heads | Forests, trees, logs, and wooded habitat |
| **Florida Sand Skink** | *Plestiodon reynoldsi* | Extremely elongated body and greatly reduced limbs | Central Florida xeric sand-ridge habitat |
| **Little Brown / Ground Skink** | *Scincella lateralis* | Tiny bronze-brown leaf-litter skink | Ground layer of woods, gardens, and leaf litter |

## How do you identify a Florida skink?

**Start with body shape, then use size, tail color, striping, scale characters, and exact locality.**

A practical first-pass key is:

1. **Extremely elongated with tiny limbs:** Florida Sand Skink.
2. **Very small, bronze-brown, leaf-litter animal:** Little Brown/Ground Skink.
3. **Small fossorial skink in sandy habitat:** Mole Skink.
4. **Five pale stripes with a blue juvenile tail:** one of the five-lined-type *Plestiodon*.
5. **Large adult male with a broad orange-red head:** Broad-headed Skink.
6. **Dark side band bordered by pale lines:** Coal Skink.

### Common Five-lined vs. Southeastern Five-lined vs. Broad-headed

Juveniles of all three can have dark bodies, five pale stripes, and bright blue tails.

Useful differences include:

- **Common Five-lined Skink:** enlarged middle row of scales beneath the tail;
- **Southeastern Five-lined Skink:** subcaudal scales are more uniform in size;
- **Broad-headed Skink:** becomes substantially larger, with mature males developing a broad orange-red head.

For deeper species-level identification, use the [Five-lined Skink Guide](/wild-skinks/five-lined-skink/) and [Broad-headed Skink Guide](/wild-skinks/broad-headed-skink/).

## What is the blue skink in Florida?

**A blue-tailed Florida skink is often a juvenile *Plestiodon*, but the exact species depends on locality and body pattern.**

Blue tails can occur in:

- juvenile Common Five-lined Skinks;
- juvenile Southeastern Five-lined Skinks;
- juvenile Broad-headed Skinks;
- some Mole Skink forms.

A blue tail is therefore a life-stage or population clue, not a complete species identification.

## What is the black skink in Florida?

**A dark or nearly black skink with pale stripes is often a juvenile five-lined-type *Plestiodon*.** Juveniles can look much darker than adults, especially when the blue tail is bright.

If the animal is tiny, uniformly bronze-brown, or extremely elongated, another Florida species is more likely.

## What is a rainbow skink in Florida?

**“Rainbow skink” is not a precise Florida native-species name.** Florida also has established introduced skinks, including colorful species such as the African Five-lined Skink (*Trachylepis quinquetaeniata*).

An unusual color pattern should be checked against both native and non-native species rather than assigned to a native skink based on a nickname.

## Where in Florida do skinks live?

**Florida skinks occupy several different habitat systems, from hardwood forest and leaf litter to coastal sand and ancient interior scrub.**

| Florida habitat/region | Skinks commonly associated with it | Main habitat clue |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Hardwood and mixed forest** | Common Five-lined, Broad-headed, Ground, localized Coal Skinks | Logs, bark, leaf litter, tree cavities, shaded cover |
| **Pine and sandy uplands** | Southeastern Five-lined and Mole Skinks | Sandy soil, surface cover, open pine habitat |
| **Central Florida sand ridges** | Florida Sand Skink and some Mole Skink populations | Deep, loose xeric sand and scrub vegetation |
| **Coastal and Keys sandy habitats** | Mole Skink subspecies | Fossorial use of coastal sand and low vegetation |

This habitat table is a regional guide rather than a county-by-county range map.

## Florida Mole Skinks and subspecies

**The Mole Skink (*Plestiodon egregius*) includes several recognized Florida subspecies, and locality is important for identification and conservation.**

Florida forms include:

- Northern Mole Skink;
- **Florida Keys Mole Skink — federally listed as Endangered effective August 20, 2026**;
- Peninsula Mole Skink;
- Cedar Key Mole Skink;
- Bluetail Mole Skink.

Tail color can vary among populations, so labels such as “red-tailed,” “pink-tailed,” or “blue-tailed” should not automatically be treated as different species.

## Florida Sand Skink: the protected sand specialist

**The Florida Sand Skink (*Plestiodon reynoldsi*) is federally listed as Threatened and is highly specialized for central Florida sand-ridge habitat.**

Key traits include:

- greatly elongated body;
- one toe on each forelimb;
- two toes on each hind limb;
- wedge-shaped snout;
- very small eyes;
- no external ear openings;
- sand-swimming locomotion.

That species' conservation status, anatomy, and restricted range belong in the dedicated [Florida Sand Skink Guide](/wild-skinks/florida-sand-skink/).

## Are Florida skinks poisonous to dogs or cats?

**Florida skinks are not considered venomous based on current evidence, but pet ingestion is not automatically risk-free.**

A cat or dog that catches or eats a wild lizard can still face:

- gastrointestinal upset;
- choking or irritation;
- parasite exposure;
- secondary chemical exposure if the prey contacted pesticides.

Cats that eat lizards in warm climates can also be exposed to **Platynosomum** liver flukes. That illness is parasite-related rather than skink poisoning.

For the full safety context, see [Are Skinks Poisonous or Dangerous?](/skinks/are-skinks-poisonous-dangerous/).

## Can you collect wild skinks in Florida?

**Collection rules depend on species and legal status.** The Florida Sand Skink is federally Threatened, and some Mole Skink populations also have conservation protections.

Do not collect a wild Florida skink until you have confirmed the current rules for that species and locality.

## Frequently asked questions

### How many native skink species live in Florida?

Seven: Coal, Mole, Common Five-lined, Southeastern Five-lined, Broad-headed, Florida Sand, and Little Brown/Ground Skinks.

### What is the blue skink in Florida?

Usually a juvenile *Plestiodon*, but several Florida species and Mole Skink forms can show colorful tails.

### What is a black skink in Florida?

Often a dark juvenile five-lined-type skink, although body shape and locality are needed to rule out other species.

### What is a rainbow skink in Florida?

It is not a precise native-species name. Florida also has introduced colorful skinks, including the African Five-lined Skink.

### Are Florida skinks poisonous?

They are not considered venomous based on current evidence. Pet ingestion can still create non-venom risks.

### Is the Florida Sand Skink endangered?

Its current federal status is **Threatened**.

### What is the difference between a Mole Skink and a Florida Sand Skink?

Mole Skinks have a more conventional skink body with four functional limbs. Florida Sand Skinks are extremely elongated and have greatly reduced one- and two-toed limbs.

### Are non-native skinks found in Florida?

Yes. Florida has established introduced skinks as well as its native species.
