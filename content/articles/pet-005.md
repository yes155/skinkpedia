# Schneider's Skink Guide: Berber Skink Care, Enclosure, Diet & Heating

**Schneider's Skink (*Eumeces schneideri*), also called the Berber Skink, is a diurnal terrestrial skink from arid and semi-arid habitats across North Africa and western/central Asia.** For a broader pet-species comparison, see the [Pet Skinks guide](/pet-skinks/). Captive care should provide substantial floor space, loose sandy soil for digging, a hot basking surface, a cooler retreat, low average humidity, UVB, and an insect-biased omnivorous diet.

## Size and lifespan

Current specialist husbandry sources place adults roughly in the **12–18 inch (30–46 cm)** total-length range, with locality and sex affecting size.

Some captive-care sources report lifespans of **15 years or more** with good care.

## Enclosure

A practical minimum for a smaller adult is about **36 × 18 × 18 inches (90 × 45 × 45 cm)**, with a **4 × 2 × 2 ft (120 × 60 × 60 cm)** enclosure or larger preferable for a full-sized adult.

The enclosure should include:

- loose diggable substrate;
- multiple hides;
- a broad warm basking surface;
- a cooler retreat;
- strong visible light and UVB;
- measured temperature and humidity;
- secure furnishings and escape prevention.

## Substrate

Use a loose, arid sandy-soil or sandy-loam substrate deep enough for the skink to dig and partially bury itself.

Avoid rigid commercial recipes. The important attributes are:

- loose enough to excavate;
- not dusty or chemically treated;
- dry overall;
- stable under furnishings;
- easy to spot-clean.

## Heating

Baines et al.'s UV-Tool lists *Eumeces schneideri* with a basking substrate surface around **40°C (104°F)** and daytime ambient temperatures around **25–30°C (77–86°F)**.

Current care sheets commonly use a basking surface around **105–115°F (40–46°C)** and a cool side around **75–85°F (24–29°C)**.

Night temperatures can fall into the high teens to low twenties Celsius where the animal is healthy and the enclosure is otherwise stable.

Use overhead heat with proper control and measure both the basking surface and the cool-side air.

## Humidity

**Keep average humidity low, generally under about 50%, while still providing normal access to drinking water and an appropriate shedding microclimate.**

Occasional humidity spikes are not the same as maintaining a humid tropical enclosure.

## UVB

Baines et al. place Schneider's Skink in **Ferguson Zone 3**, supporting access to relatively strong UVB compared with shade-dwelling skinks.

Build a measured gradient rather than choosing a bulb percentage in isolation. Provide bright light, UVB over the basking side, and meaningful shade.

## Diet

**Schneider's Skinks are omnivorous with a strong insect component.**

Offer:

- varied captive feeder insects as the main animal-food component;
- appropriate plant foods supported by species care references;
- clean water.

Avoid forcing a universal 80/20 ratio when intake can vary with age, season, and individual condition.

## Handling

Some individuals become tolerant of calm handling, but temperament varies.

Allow new animals to settle, support the body fully, and do not restrain the tail.

## Frequently asked questions

### How big do Schneider's Skinks get?

Roughly 12–18 inches (30–46 cm) total length in current specialist care references.

### What enclosure size do they need?

About 36 × 18 × 18 inches is a practical minimum for a smaller adult; 4 × 2 × 2 ft or larger is preferable for a full-sized animal.

### How hot should the basking area be?

Around 40–46°C (104–115°F) at the basking **surface**, with a cooler side around 24–29°C (75–85°F).

### What humidity do they need?

A low average humidity, generally under about 50%.

### Do Schneider's Skinks need UVB?

Yes. Baines et al.'s UV-Tool places the species in Ferguson Zone 3.

### What do Schneider's Skinks eat?

An insect-biased omnivorous diet.
