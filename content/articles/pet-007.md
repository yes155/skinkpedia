# Pink-Tongued Skink Guide: Care, Diet, Enclosure & Lifespan

**The Pink-Tongued Skink (*Cyclodomorphus gerrardii*) is a slender, semi-arboreal Australian skink specialized for eating snails and slugs.** Compare its long-term commitment and care style with other species in the [Pet Skinks guide](/pet-skinks/). Adults are roughly 35–45 cm long, and current specialist husbandry guidance places captive lifespan around 15–20 years.

Its enclosure should combine ground cover with stable climbing structure rather than treating it like a heavy terrestrial blue-tongue.

## Size, lifespan and activity

Current specialist guidance gives:

| Attribute | Practical value |
| --- | --- |
| **Adult total length** | About **35–45 cm (14–18 in)** |
| **Captive lifespan** | About **15–20 years** |
| **Activity** | Mainly crepuscular/nocturnal, with some daytime basking |
| **Lifestyle** | Semi-arboreal |
| **Diet specialization** | Snails and slugs are central |

Juveniles are strongly banded and commonly have a blue tongue that changes toward pink as they mature.

## Enclosure size

A current welfare-oriented recommendation is **at least about 120 × 60 × 90 cm (48 × 24 × 36 in)** for one adult.

Use both floor area and height with:

- broad stable branches;
- cork tubes;
- elevated hides;
- ground-level cover;
- dense foliage;
- safe climbing routes;
- clean water;
- good ventilation.

## Temperature

Current Herpeton Academy guidance recommends:

- **basking surface:** about **32–35°C (90–95°F)**;
- **warm-side air:** about **24–29°C (75–84°F)**;
- **cool side:** about **22–25°C (72–77°F)**;
- **night:** about **18–22°C (64–72°F)**.

The enclosure should provide both warm elevated choices and cooler shaded retreats.

## Humidity

**Maintain roughly 60–80% humidity with strong ventilation and some drying between moisture cycles.**

A humid enclosure should still have moving air and drainage; continuously saturated substrate is not the goal.

## UVB

Current specialist guidance uses a low-to-moderate linear UVB gradient around **UVI 1–3 at accessible basking areas**, with deep shade available.

Fixture output should be measured where possible because distance and mesh alter UVI substantially.

## Diet

**Snails and slugs should form the core of the feeding plan.**

Other foods can include selected invertebrates and species-appropriate complete foods where a reputable care source supports them.

Use captive or food-safe molluscs from controlled sources rather than collecting prey from areas exposed to pesticides or unknown parasite risks.

## Handling

Pink-Tongued Skinks are often calmer than many small, fast skinks, but temperament varies.

Support the entire body, allow the animal to grip naturally, and avoid pulling it from branches or clothing.

## Frequently asked questions

### How big do Pink-Tongued Skinks get?

About 35–45 cm (14–18 in) total length.

### How long do Pink-Tongued Skinks live?

Current specialist guidance gives roughly 15–20 years in captivity.

### What size enclosure do they need?

At least about 120 × 60 × 90 cm for one adult, with substantial climbing structure.

### What humidity do they need?

Roughly 60–80% with strong ventilation.

### What UVI should they have?

Current Herpeton Academy guidance uses approximately UVI 1–3 at the basking level, with shade available.

### What do Pink-Tongued Skinks eat?

Snails and slugs are the core natural food, supplemented with other suitable foods where supported.
