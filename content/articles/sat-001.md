# Skink Meaning: Definition, Pronunciation, Etymology & Other Uses

**A skink is primarily a lizard in the family Scincidae.** The word is pronounced **/skɪŋk/**, rhyming with *think* and *drink*. English also preserves older, unrelated senses of *skink*: a chiefly dialectal verb meaning **to draw, pour, or serve a drink**, and a Scots culinary noun referring to **soup or broth**, best known today from *Cullen skink*.

The word therefore has three main contexts: **reptile → historical verb → Scottish food term**. The reptile is the standard modern meaning.

## What does skink mean?

### Skink as a reptile

In modern standard English, **skink** is a countable noun for a member of the lizard family **Scincidae**.

Skinks are a diverse lizard group, so the dictionary definition should not be reduced to one body form. Many species have:

- smooth, overlapping scales;
- elongated bodies;
- relatively short limbs;
- reduced limbs in some burrowing lineages.

Other skinks are robust, fully limbed, arboreal, terrestrial, or rock-dwelling. For the animal itself—types, habitat, size, behavior, and biology—see the [main skink guide](/skinks/).

### Skink as a verb

**Skink can also be a chiefly dialectal verb meaning to draw, pour out, or serve a drink.**

The regular forms are:

- **skink**
- **skinks**
- **skinked**
- **skinking**

This historical verb is separate from the reptile noun even though the modern spelling is the same.

### Skink as a Scottish food word

In Scots, **skink** has also been used for **soup or broth**. That culinary sense survives most visibly in **Cullen skink**, the Scottish smoked-haddock soup.

For the dish rather than the lizard, see the [Cullen skink recipe and history](/extras/cullen-skink-recipe/).

## How do you pronounce skink?

**Skink is pronounced /skɪŋk/.**

A simple pronunciation guide is:

> **skink** = “skink,” rhyming with **think**, **drink**, **link**, and **pink**

It is one syllable.

## What is the plural of skink?

The regular plural is **skinks**.

Examples:

- “A skink was hiding under the log.”
- “Several skinks occur in this region.”

## Where does the word skink come from?

The **reptile noun** and the **drink-serving verb** have different histories.

### Origin of the reptile noun

The English reptile word entered the language in the late sixteenth century. Major dictionaries trace it through:

**Greek *skinkos* → Latin *scincus* → English *skink***

The scientific family name **Scincidae** reflects the same classical word family.

### Origin of the verb

The dialectal verb meaning **to pour or serve drink** comes through Middle English from a Germanic source, commonly connected with Middle Dutch **schenken** and related English/Germanic words for pouring or serving drink.

This is a different etymological path from the Greek-Latin reptile noun.

### Origin of the Scottish soup sense

The Scots culinary noun has its own history. Scots dictionaries record **skink** as soup or broth and connect older forms with words for **shin/hough**, reflecting an earlier meat-broth sense.

That is why the modern phrase **Cullen skink** contains the same spelling as the reptile word without referring to a lizard.

| **Zoology** | Noun | A lizard in the family Scincidae | Standard modern meaning |

## Does skink have a slang meaning?

**There is no single well-established modern slang definition accepted across major dictionaries.** Online sources sometimes use *skink* or *skinking* playfully for sneaking, slipping away, or acting “skink-like,” but these meanings are inconsistent and highly context-dependent.

For the difference between verified dictionary meanings and unstable online usage, see [Skink Slang Meaning](/meaning/skink-slang-meaning/).

## Is “skink” another word for lizard?

**Skink is a type of lizard, not a synonym for every lizard.**

All skinks are lizards, but many lizards—geckos, anoles, iguanas, monitors, alligator lizards, and others—are not skinks.

A more precise zoological related term is **scincid**, meaning a member of or relating to the family Scincidae.

## Frequently asked questions

### What do you mean by skink?

Usually, a skink is a lizard in the family Scincidae. The word also has older dialectal and Scottish culinary meanings.

### What is the dictionary definition of skink?

The primary modern definition is a lizard belonging to the family Scincidae.

### What is a skink in English?

In standard English, it is a Scincidae lizard. Historically, *skink* can also be a verb meaning to pour or serve a drink.

### What does skink mean in Scottish usage?

In Scots, *skink* can refer to soup or broth. The best-known surviving example is Cullen skink.

### Does skink mean to pour out?

Yes. Dictionaries record a chiefly dialectal verb *skink* meaning to draw, pour out, or serve a drink.

### How do you say skink?

Pronounce it **/skɪŋk/**, rhyming with *think* and *drink*.

### What is another word for skink?

**Scincid** is the closest precise zoological term. **Lizard** is broader and includes many non-skink families.

### Why are they called skinks?

The reptile name ultimately comes from Greek *skinkos* through Latin *scincus*.

### Is skink a noun or a verb?

Both. It is primarily a noun for the reptile, but a historical/dialectal verb *skink* means to pour or serve drink.

### Is Cullen skink made from skinks?

No. Cullen skink is a Scottish smoked-haddock soup. The food name is unrelated to the reptile family.
