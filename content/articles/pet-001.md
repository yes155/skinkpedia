# Fire Skink Guide: Togo Fire Skink Care, Enclosure, Diet & Size

**The Fire Skink (*Mochlus fernandi*) is a terrestrial, burrowing West African skink that reaches roughly 12–15 inches (30–38 cm) total length.** To compare Fire Skinks with other commonly kept species before choosing one, use the [Pet Skinks guide](/pet-skinks/). Good captive care is built around floor space, 4–6 inches of moisture-retentive substrate, a measured thermal gradient, moderate-to-high humidity, UVB access, and a varied insect diet.

For general principles, see the [skink care guide](/skink-care/), [skink enclosure setup guide](/skink-care/enclosure-setup/), and [skink diet guide](/skink-care/diet/).

## Fire skink size and lifespan

Current species-specific husbandry guidance gives:

| Attribute | Practical value |
| --- | --- |
| **Adult total length** | About **12–15 in (30–38 cm)** |
| **Potential captive lifespan** | About **15–20 years** with good care |
| **Lifestyle** | Terrestrial and burrowing |
| **Diet type** | Insectivorous |

Comparative morphology research also reports an average snout-to-vent length around 125 mm for *M. fernandi*. SVL excludes the tail, so it should not be confused with total adult length.

## Enclosure size

**A single adult should have at least about 36 × 18 × 18 inches (90 × 45 × 45 cm), with larger floor space preferred.**

The enclosure should provide:

- a warm zone and a cooler retreat;
- enough substrate depth for burrowing;
- at least two secure hiding areas;
- visual cover;
- measured humidity;
- safe overhead heat and UVB;
- secure doors or lid.

Glass, PVC, or sealed wood can work if the finished enclosure maintains the required environment.

## Substrate and burrowing

**Provide about 4–6 inches (10–15 cm) of loose, moisture-retentive substrate.**

Suitable categories include tropical soil-style substrates and soil/coconut-fiber blends that hold some moisture without becoming waterlogged. The lower layers can remain slightly moister while the surface is allowed to dry somewhat between misting cycles.

Avoid dusty, sharp, chemically treated, or otherwise unsuitable substrates. The useful rule is not that one accidental grain is “fatal,” but that the enclosure should support normal burrowing while minimizing ingestion, respiratory, and hygiene risks.

## Temperature and humidity

Current ReptiFiles guidance recommends:

- **basking area:** about **92–96°F (33–35.5°C)**;
- **cool zone:** about **75–85°F (24–29°C)**;
- **night:** about **70–75°F (21–24°C)**;
- **average humidity:** about **60–70%**.

Measure the basking area and cool zone independently. Use thermostatic or proportional control where appropriate, and use a digital hygrometer rather than judging humidity by touch.

## UVB and lighting

Fire Skinks are diurnal and benefit from bright daytime lighting.

ReptiFiles recommends a measured UV gradient reaching roughly **UVI 3.0–4.0 at the basking area**, with substantially lower exposure and shade elsewhere. Fixture choice, mesh, distance, and enclosure height all change output, so use a UV meter where possible rather than treating bulb percentage as the biological target.

A roughly 12-hour day/night cycle is a practical baseline.

## Diet

**Fire Skinks are insectivorous.** Feed a varied rotation of appropriately sized captive invertebrates such as:

- crickets;
- roaches;
- grasshoppers/locusts where available;
- larvae and worms used as part of a varied feeder rotation.

Adults generally need fewer meals than juveniles. ReptiFiles uses about **two feedings per week for adults**, with younger animals fed more frequently.

Gut-load feeder insects and use calcium/multivitamin products according to the product label, UVB provision, age, and veterinary guidance rather than following an arbitrary universal dusting schedule.

## Water and humidity management

Provide clean drinking water at all times. Misting can be used to maintain the humidity cycle, but ventilation should still allow the enclosure to avoid becoming stagnant or continuously saturated.

## Handling

Fire Skinks are fast, cover-oriented animals. Some become tolerant of calm interaction, but handling temperament varies.

When handling is necessary:

- approach calmly;
- scoop from below;
- support the whole body;
- keep the animal close to a secure surface;
- never restrain it by the tail.

## Frequently asked questions

### How big do Fire Skinks get?

About 12–15 inches (30–38 cm) total length.

### What size enclosure does a Fire Skink need?

A current species-specific baseline is about 36 × 18 × 18 inches (90 × 45 × 45 cm) for one adult, with larger floor space preferred.

### How deep should Fire Skink substrate be?

About 4–6 inches (10–15 cm) of loose, moisture-retentive burrowing substrate.

### What temperature should a Fire Skink have?

A basking area around 92–96°F (33–35.5°C), with a cooler zone around 75–85°F (24–29°C).

### What humidity do Fire Skinks need?

About 60–70% average humidity, with good ventilation and normal drying between misting cycles.

### Do Fire Skinks need UVB?

Current care guidance recommends UVB. ReptiFiles targets roughly UVI 3.0–4.0 at the basking area, with shade available.

### What do Fire Skinks eat?

Primarily varied captive feeder invertebrates.
