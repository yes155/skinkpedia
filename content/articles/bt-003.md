# Blue-Tongue Skink Enclosure: Size, Substrate, Heating, UVB & Humidity

**A blue-tongue skink enclosure should provide at least 120 × 60 × 60 cm of secure terrestrial space for one animal, with a controlled warm-to-cool gradient, UVB exposure, deep substrate, hides, and species-appropriate humidity.** The enclosure should be fully assembled and tested before the skink arrives.

This guide focuses on **physical installation and setup**. For routine husbandry, return to the [blue-tongue skink care guide](/pet-skinks/blue-tongue-skink/care/).

## Minimum enclosure size

**The RSPCA advises a minimum vivarium of 120 cm long × 60 cm deep × 60 cm high for a single blue-tongue skink.**

The enclosure should be:

- secure against escape;
- made from a solid, sealed material;
- well ventilated;
- large enough to create a thermogradient;
- able to hold a thick substrate layer;
- positioned away from drafts, direct sunlight, and uncontrolled heat.

PVC, sealed wood, and suitable glass-fronted designs can all work if the finished enclosure safely maintains the required climate. The material itself is less important than the environmental outcome.

## Heating and temperature gradient

**Create the basking zone at one end of the enclosure and keep the opposite end cooler.**

The RSPCA baseline is:

- **basking zone: 30–32°C**;
- **up to 35°C for younger skinks**;
- **cool end: about 22–26°C**;
- **night: about 20–22°C**.

Use a suitable wattage halogen heat lamp or a ceramic non-light-emitting heater where appropriate. All heat sources should be controlled by a thermostat.

Do not copy a fixed wattage from another enclosure. Choose the heater that safely produces the correct measured temperature in your enclosure.

## Guarding heat sources

**Heat lamps should be guarded when the animal could contact them or when breakage could cause injury.** The RSPCA specifically recommends guards to reduce burns or injury if a bulb shatters.

Place thermostat and thermometer probes so they measure the environment the skink actually experiences rather than a remote part of the enclosure.

## Measuring temperature

**Use independent digital thermometers to verify both the basking area and the cool end.** An infrared thermometer can also help check surface temperatures.

The RSPCA recommends checking maximum and minimum temperatures daily because thermostat settings alone are not proof that the enclosure is correct.

## Basking surface

**A broad, stable basking surface helps the skink use the heated zone safely.** The RSPCA suggests a stone around **30 cm across** and advises preventing the animal’s back from getting closer than about **25 cm to the heat source** in its example setup.

Treat those distances as part of the specific lamp-and-enclosure configuration rather than a universal rule for every fixture. Actual UV and heat output still need to be measured.

## UVB lighting

**Blue-tongue skinks should have a UVB photogradient with stronger exposure near the basking area and shade elsewhere.**

For a 60 cm-high vivarium, the RSPCA example recommends:

- a **6% UVB tube**;
- roughly **half the length of the vivarium**;
- fixed toward the hot end;
- used with an appropriate reflector;
- output verified with a UV Index meter.

The target exposure is **UVI 3.0–5.0 in the basking zone, grading to 0 in shade**.

Do not choose a UVB percentage or mounting distance by brand label alone. Enclosure height, lamp output, barriers, reflector, and distance all affect the result.

## Substrate depth

**Blue-tongue skinks benefit from a thick substrate layer because they naturally burrow.** The RSPCA recommends approximately **4 inches (about 10 cm)** of substrate.

Its care sheet suggests:

- organic natural soil; or
- a soil/sand mix.

It specifically advises against indigestible substrates such as:

- calci-sand;
- beech chips;
- corn cob granules;
- crushed walnut shells.

The concern is ingestion and gastrointestinal blockage.

## Humidity

**Humidity should be matched to the exact blue-tongue species or locality.** The RSPCA gives a broad genus-level range of approximately **40–90% depending on species**.

Use a hygrometer at the cool end and adjust the enclosure through:

- ventilation;
- substrate moisture;
- spraying where appropriate;
- local humid refuges.

Do not use one Northern or Indonesian humidity value across every *Tiliqua* without a taxon-specific source.

## Hides and enrichment

**Provide secure hiding places in both warm and cool zones so the skink does not have to choose between shelter and thermoregulation.**

The RSPCA also recommends:

- stones and branches for climbing or exploration;
- appropriately sized hides;
- a moist hide containing damp moss;
- a large, shallow water dish.

All heavy furnishings should be stable so digging cannot undermine them.

## Water

**Place a large, shallow dish of clean water in the cool end.** Replace the water daily and whenever it is soiled.

## Run the setup before the skink arrives

**The RSPCA recommends running the complete vivarium for at least one week before introducing the animal.** This gives you time to identify temperature, lighting, thermostat, humidity, or equipment faults before the skink depends on the system.

During that test period, verify:

- basking temperature;
- cool-end temperature;
- night temperature;
- UV exposure;
- humidity;
- thermostat function;
- lamp guards;
- escape security.

## Frequently asked questions
### What size enclosure does an adult blue-tongue skink need?

At least 120 × 60 × 60 cm for one animal in the RSPCA care sheet.

### How deep should the substrate be?

The RSPCA recommends about 4 inches (10 cm) for burrowing.

### What UVI should a blue-tongue skink receive?

UVI 3.0–5.0 at the basking zone, grading to zero in shade.

### What temperature should the basking spot be?

About 30–32°C, up to 35°C for younger skinks.

### Should blue-tongue skinks be housed together?

The RSPCA advises keeping them separately because territorial conflict can lead to bites and serious injury.
