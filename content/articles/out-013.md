# Skink Reproduction: Eggs, Mating, Pregnancy, Live Birth & Hatching

**Skinks use both major reptile reproductive modes: many species lay eggs, while many others give birth to live young.** Comparative reproductive literature estimates roughly **55% oviparous and 45% viviparous**, although the exact percentage changes as taxonomy and species counts are updated.

This guide follows **mating → oviparity/viviparity → incubation/gestation → parental care**. For the broader anatomy and evolutionary context, see the [Skink Biology guide](/skink-biology/).

## Do skinks lay eggs or give live birth?

**Both.**

Examples of egg-laying skinks include many *Plestiodon* species such as the Five-lined Skink. Blue-tongue skinks in the genus *Tiliqua* are familiar examples of live-bearing skinks.

The diversity is unusually important in Scincidae because live birth has evolved repeatedly within the family.

## How often has live birth evolved in skinks?

Comparative evolutionary research identifies **at least 22 independent origins of viviparity** within skinks.

That number should be interpreted as an evolutionary estimate from the source framework rather than a permanent species count. Phylogenies and taxonomic boundaries continue to be refined.

## What do skink eggs look like?

Egg appearance varies by species.

Many egg-laying skinks produce small, pale, flexible or parchment-shelled eggs and place them in protected microhabitats.

One documented example is the invasive Plague Skink, whose eggs are approximately **10 × 6 mm** and may occur in communal nests. That measurement belongs to that species and should not be transferred to every skink.

## Where do skinks lay eggs?

Depending on species, females may use:

- cavities beneath logs;
- spaces under rocks;
- loose soil;
- rotting wood;
- protected communal nesting sites;
- other humid or thermally suitable microhabitats.

There is no universal “skink nest” design.

## How do skinks mate?

**Courtship and mating behavior vary widely across the family.**

Chemical cues and tongue-flicking are important in many skinks, and males of some species follow females or use physical contact during courtship.

## What does a pregnant or gravid skink look like?

A female carrying eggs or embryos may become broader through the abdomen and gain mass, but appearance alone cannot reliably confirm pregnancy.

Possible changes can include:

- abdominal enlargement;
- body-mass increase;
- altered basking or shelter use;
- feeding changes.

These signs are not diagnostic. Avoid repeatedly palpating or squeezing the abdomen.

## Maternal care in egg-laying skinks

**Maternal care is documented in several skink lineages.**

### Prairie Skinks

The current scientific name is ***Plestiodon septentrionalis***, formerly *Eumeces septentrionalis*.

Published evidence describes females:

- coiling around eggs;
- maintaining nest conditions;
- remaining with hatchlings briefly;
- sometimes attending unrelated clutches or young, an example of alloparental behavior.

### Long-tailed Sun Skink

The current name is ***Eutropis longicaudata***, formerly *Mabuya longicaudata*.

Published research documents maternal attendance and active defense of eggs against egg-eating snakes.

These examples show that “reptiles abandon their eggs” is not a safe generalization for skinks.

## How long do skink eggs take to hatch?

**There is no single skink incubation period.** Development time depends on species, incubation temperature, moisture, and other environmental conditions.

Published incubation times vary substantially among species, so a broad family-wide day range should not be treated as a universal rule.

Use a species-specific source for exact incubation time.

## How do you hatch skink eggs?

A family-wide article should not provide one temperature-and-moisture recipe because skink eggs differ by species.

For captive breeding:

1. identify the exact species;
2. use species-specific incubation data;
3. avoid rotating an egg after orientation has been established unless the species protocol says otherwise;
4. maintain the temperature and moisture regime supported for that species;
5. seek experienced veterinary or breeder guidance when the species has limited captive-breeding data.

Do not collect or incubate eggs from protected wild species.

## How do live-bearing skinks nourish embryos?

Viviparous skinks retain developing embryos internally. Placental complexity varies widely across Scincidae: some species rely more heavily on yolk, while others have more developed maternal-fetal nutrient transfer.

Blue-tongue skinks are live-bearing, but their reproductive biology should not be generalized to every viviparous skink.

See the [Blue-Tongue Skink profile](/pet-skinks/blue-tongue-skink/) for a familiar species example.

## How many babies do skinks have?

Clutch and litter size varies enormously by species.

Some skinks produce only one or a few large young; others produce larger clutches or litters. Avoid publishing “1 to 20+” as though it describes a normal range for every species.

## Frequently asked questions
### Do all skinks lay eggs?

No. The published evidence estimates roughly 55% of skinks are egg-laying and 45% live-bearing.

### Do Blue-Tongue Skinks lay eggs?

No. *Tiliqua* blue-tongue skinks are live-bearing.

### What do skink eggs look like?

Many are small and pale with a flexible or parchment-like shell, but egg size and appearance are species-specific.

### How big are skink eggs?

There is no universal size. Plague Skink eggs are about 10 × 6 mm in the published evidence, while other species differ.

### How long do skink eggs take to hatch?

It depends on species and incubation conditions. Use an exact species source rather than a family-wide day count.

### How can you tell if a skink is pregnant?

Abdominal enlargement and weight gain may occur, but appearance alone does not confirm pregnancy.

### Do skinks guard their eggs?

Some do. Maternal attendance is well documented in several *Plestiodon* species and in the Long-tailed Sun Skink.

### Do skinks care for babies after hatching?

Most young become independent quickly, but some species show brief post-hatching attendance or other parental behaviors.
