# Garden Skinks: Identification, Benefits and How to Make a Skink-Friendly Yard

**Skinks can be useful backyard wildlife because many small garden species feed on insects and other invertebrates, while gardens can provide shelter, basking sites, food and nesting cover.** A skink-friendly yard is usually built around **rocks and logs → leaf litter and ground cover → native plants → reduced pesticide exposure → safer pet management**.

“Garden skink” can mean two things: in Australia it can refer specifically to *Lampropholis* species such as *Lampropholis guichenoti*; elsewhere, people often use it generically for any skink found around gardens and houses. For the family-wide overview, see [What Is a Skink?](/skinks/).

## Is it good to have skinks in your yard?

Usually, yes. Many species consume insects and other small invertebrates and form part of the local food web.

They may eat beetles, crickets, spiders, moths and larvae, flies, worms, and snails or slugs in some larger species.

It is too strong to claim that skinks will eliminate pest infestations or replace all pest management.

## What does a Common Garden Skink look like?

If the phrase refers specifically to the Australian **Common Garden Skink / Garden Sunskink (*Lampropholis guichenoti*)**, it is a small gray-brown, olive-brown or bronze skink with darker striping and a maximum total length around **10 cm** in Museums Victoria material.

Outside Australia, “garden skink” may mean a different local species.

## How to make a garden skink-friendly

Wildlife guidance recommends:
- flat rocks in sunny areas;
- logs, large branches, bark or roof tiles;
- leaf litter and coarse mulch;
- native grasses and ground covers;
- dense planting near shelter;
- shallow clean water with easy exit points.

The goal is habitat variety, not one exact layout.

## Add rocks and logs

Keep heavy rocks stable, provide shelter near vegetation and avoid collecting rocks or logs from protected natural habitat.

## Keep leaf litter and mulch

Leaf litter provides hiding cover, cooler microclimates, prey habitat and nesting opportunities for some species.

## Provide water safely

Use a shallow ground-level dish with texture and stones or sticks that provide an easy exit. There is no universal **0.5-inch maximum depth** for all garden skinks, so that rule has been removed.

## Reduce pesticide exposure

Reducing broad pesticide use can preserve invertebrate prey and reduce non-target exposure.

Snail and slug baits require caution. **Metaldehyde can poison non-target animals and wildlife.** Do not leave exposed chemical bait where pets or wildlife can reach it.

## Keep cats and dogs from hunting skinks

Useful steps include:
- keep cats indoors or in secure outdoor enclosures;
- supervise dogs where lizards are active;
- provide dense shelter;
- avoid encouraging pets to chase wildlife.

## What if skinks are entering the house?

This guide focuses on garden coexistence; indoor exclusion is covered separately. See [How to Keep Skinks Out of the House](/backyard-skinks/keep-skinks-out-house/).

For droppings, see [What Does Skink Poop Look Like?](/backyard-skinks/skink-poop-feces/).

## Can you keep a garden skink as a pet?

Do not assume a wild backyard skink can legally or appropriately be collected. Wildlife rules vary by location.

For captive species, see the [pet skink selection guide](/pet-skinks/).

## Frequently asked questions
### Is it good to have skinks in your yard?
Yes. Many garden skinks eat insects and other invertebrates and are a normal part of the local food web.

### How do I attract skinks to my garden?
Provide stable rocks and logs, leaf litter, native ground cover, safe shallow water and reduced pesticide exposure.

### What do garden skinks eat?
Many small species eat insects, spiders, larvae, worms and other small invertebrates.

### What is a Common Garden Skink?
In Australia, the name often refers to *Lampropholis guichenoti*. Elsewhere, “garden skink” can be a generic backyard term.

### Are garden skinks poisonous to cats or dogs?
Skinks are not considered venomous based on current evidence. Pets that eat wild lizards can still develop digestive problems or acquire parasites.

### Can I keep a garden skink as a pet?
Check local wildlife law first. Leaving wild skinks in the garden and choosing a legal captive-bred pet species is usually the better route.

### How do I stop skinks entering my house?
Use humane exclusion and seal entry gaps. See [How to Keep Skinks Out of the House](/backyard-skinks/keep-skinks-out-house/).
